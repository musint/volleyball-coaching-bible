# Age Guide: 14s, late-fundamentals → specialization onset

This guide is the operational layer for the wiki's primary coaching context: **14U girls club volleyball at a nationally-recognized program**. It assumes you have already read [[age-lens-14u]], which sets the developmental philosophy, age-appropriate ceilings, and source canon for this age band [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules][usav-2009-cap-game-like-training]. This guide goes one layer deeper: match-spec milestones, position-by-position skill targets, [[munciana-volleyball-club]]-style operational depth on practice design, and the promotion-criteria language that drives the [[tryout-rubric-14u]] scoring scale. Adjacent age-guides are [[13s]] (the runway in) and [[15s]] (the runway out).

## Overview & development phase

Phase: **late-fundamentals → specialization onset.** A 14U athlete in their second year of organized 6s should be running stable forearm-pass mechanics, contacting overhead sets with hand-hinge rather than chest-trap, executing a reliable float serve to zone (jump-float emerging in rotation), and beginning to defend live attacks with a recognizable platform-and-pursuit identity [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals][aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge][aoc-2024-kids-serving-fundamentals]. The fundamentals are now being *consolidated under tournament pressure* while position identity emerges underneath. By late season a coach should be able to name, for almost every athlete, a primary position and a secondary position they could legitimately play at 15s.

The developmental load is heavy and uneven: pre-adolescent to mid-adolescent growth is rapid and asymmetric, and coordination, strength, and proprioception shift week to week [till-2022-ltad-practitioner-knowledge]. What 14s adds beyond [[13s]] is **specialization onset**: a deliberate narrowing of the developmental fan-out. The libero-candidate pool starts running serve-receive-heavy reps separately from the OH pool [aoc-2024-kiraly-training-libero]; the setter pool gets dedicated tempo work [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-attack-tempo]; the middle pool starts learning the three-tempo footwork even if Tempo 1 is months away [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-tempo]. This is the age where Hugh McCutcheon's "generalized specialists" pipeline begins meaningfully, broadly skilled athletes with one or two emerging strengths, not early-narrowing single-position specialists [usav-2026-hp-clinic-lessons-shared].

## Skill milestones

### Passing
- **Milestone:** Forearm pass is reliable from a tossed serve and a moderate-pace standing-float serve, and *introducing* under live tougher serves [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals].
- **Anchor framework:** Kiraly's 4-keys, stopped and balanced; thumbs together with platform formed late; angle set with the shoulders, not the arms; simple one-way motion through the ball [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass].
- **Coaching cue layer:** Lingenfelter's quiet-platform / pass-and-cover micro-skills as named drill targets, not assumptions, balance trained on one foot, quiet platform via one-armed and lateral-shuffle reps, pass-and-cover trained by sprinting to a designated spot after the contact rather than admiring the pass [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing].
- **Late-14s technical target:** athletes should hop back and take a jamming ball low on the platform rather than reaching up [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing].
- **Overhead pass:** introduced for free balls and high-arcing serves; the [[passing-overhead]] decision (forearm vs. hands) becomes a deliberate read, not an accident.
- **Failure mode:** athlete steps into the ball with arms first instead of stopping balanced and forming the platform second.
- See [[passing-cues]].

### Setting
- **Milestone:** Setter candidates demonstrate hand-hinge and side-of-ball contact from day one, bad mechanics ingrained at 14 are extremely hard to retrain at 17 [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge][aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals].
- **Late-14s deliverables:** stable outside set off a reasonable pass, competent backset without telegraphing, workable jump-set from in-system passes (see [[setting-jump]]).
- **Tempo vocabulary (install, not execute):** a 14s setter should *understand* Lingenfelter's three-tempo framework: Tempo 3 (high to the pins, plant left foot after the ball leaves the setter's hands), Tempo 2 (plant while the ball is in the setter's hands), Tempo 1 (middle airborne while the ball is in the setter's hands) [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-tempo][aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-attack-tempo]. For the contested-school distribution philosophy: Italian / Brazilian / Russian / Kiraly / GMS, see [[setting-distribution]]; at 14s, the GMS pass-quality-driven framework is the install default, with Italian / Brazilian tempo-first influences introduced via Lingenfelter's Tempo 2 reps.
- **Match execution ceiling:** Tempo 3 default; Tempo 2 in rotation for advanced setter-middle pairs at top-club 14s by mid-season; Tempo 1 remains 15s+ execution territory [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-tempo].
- **Failure mode:** chest-trap on the overhead set, setter contacts low and pushes through with chest instead of hand-hinge with side-of-ball contact.
- **Named drill target:** [[target-setting]] with hands-only constraint to enforce hand-hinge.
- See [[setting-cues]].

### Hitting
- **Approach milestone:** Three-step approach is reliable from in-system passes, with Schmidt's "start slow, accelerate the last two steps" tempo cue producing a visible deceleration-then-explosion pattern on the penultimate step [aoc-2024-attack-approach][aoc-2021-teach-spiking].
- **Four-step install, late-14s, top-club only.** The three-step stays the teaching default from 12s through 14s. Barnard's modern four-step adds one directional first step ahead of it, and that extra step is what buys the "fast-fast-faster" acceleration profile: more room to reach top speed before takeoff, and more time to adjust to a set that is not where it should be [aoc-2023-barnard-aggressive-approach]. The tradeoff runs both ways, and Skinner states it cleanly: the three-step teaches relaxation and weight transfer, the four-step builds momentum and covers more court, and both have to survive an imperfect set [aoc-2023-skinner-broad-jump-elimination]. Install it late-14s as a layered transition rather than a replacement, so the athlete keeps the three-step as her pressure fallback and adds the directional step first on high-ball outside sets, where there is time to use it. It becomes the standard at [[15s]]. [[approach-and-swing]] carries the install: run it with a tape mark for the new first step and let the athlete choose three or four steps per rep until the four-step wins on its own.
- **Arm-swing framework:** MacDonald's torque-and-high-hand emphasis paired with Mattox's Sight-90 / Elbow-Lead / Lift-and-Whip keywords [aoc-2024-attack-arm-mechanics].
- **Late-14s shot inventory:** the [[hitting-shot-selection]] trio (big cross, line, tip) is in the inventory off a workable in-system set.
- **Back-row attack repped, not yet match-default:** pipe (Zone 6) and OH back-row (Zone 1) attempted in controlled drills with setter leading the hitter from in-system passes [aoc-2023-back-row-attacking-basics]. Top-club 14s rosters introduce pipe in match play late season as a high-ball variation; mid-set staple is 15s+.
- **Female-specific approach coaching:** women approach at slower speeds and use shorter penultimate steps than men in matched-elite samples, and strength and power do not fully explain the gap [fuchs-2019-male-female-spike-biomechanics]. Approach velocity and penultimate-step length are therefore *technical* leverage points, coachable at 14s from cue rather than gated behind the weight room.
- **Failure mode:** penultimate step too long, plant too far from the ball, OH approaches into the net rather than parallel to it.
- **Anti-cue:** do not say "snap your wrist", over-emphasizes wrist instead of the arm-and-hand dynamic with high-hand torque.
- See [[hitting-cues]].

### Blocking
- **Primary footwork pattern:** Static-and-shuffle for pin blockers as entry; full swing-block execution (Rosen footwork + jump) emerging for middles at top-club 14s by mid-season [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners][aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking].
- **Swing-block install:** Rosen's 10-foot-line progression as the curriculum, steps installed without jump, jump added once footwork is automatic [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners]. Top-club middles complete the progression (footwork + jump) by mid-season. **Pin blockers stay footwork-only through the whole 14s year; their jump-add is held to late 15s** [aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking]. Drill: [[block-footwork-ladder]] for the dry-footwork volume, [[swing-block-shuffle]] for middles only once the jump is in (it is an HS-tagged drill, so run it as the top-club middles' extension block, not as a roster-wide rep).
- **Coaching anchor:** hand-press emphasis with net penetration, and the pin-blocker closing stance with the outside foot forward so the hips rotate back square to the net. That stance is program blocking convention [unsourced]. It is a different cue from Munciana's "outside foot high," which is a perimeter-defense rule for back-court defenders and lives in the Defense section below [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Ceiling lifted at top-club 14s:** full swing-block execution belongs in match play for middles once the jump-add is reliable [aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking]; bunch-read concept is introducible as a tactical layer against opponents whose middle threat is real [aoc-2026-bunch-read-blocking]. Bunch-read execution as a default scheme remains 15s+ territory [aoc-2023-swing-blocking-when].
- **Critical injury caveat:** Zahradnik's female-specific landing study found run-back-style block landings (where a blocker breaks down and sprints back to play backcourt defense) produced significantly higher knee-valgus moments than step-back landings (p=0.019) [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus].
- **Default landing pattern:** teach the controlled stick or step-back landing as the default at 14s; treat run-back as a recovery-only pattern, not a coachable habit [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus].
- **Failure mode:** run-back landing as default, highest knee-valgus moment landing pattern. Hard rule: any athlete with prior knee-pain history practices stick-only landings until cleared.
- See [[blocking-cues]].

### Serving
- **Headline milestone:** Jump-float in rotation; standing-float reliable as secondary.
- **Primary serve at top-club 14s, jump-float in rotation.** The install target by mid-season is 2-3 jump-float servers contributing to the rotation; toss consistency is the gate, not contact. Mattox's Lift/Step/Swing extends across the approach step into a controlled in-air contact [aoc-2025-float-serve-3-keys][aoc-2024-float-serve-toss]. The pro-women's-game empirical anchor: jump-float comprises **86%** of elite women's pro serves vs jump-topspin's 12%, in a full-season Polish Tauron Liga study [pawlik-2024-women-mens-serve-effectiveness]; rotation use of jump-float at 14s is the developmental on-ramp to that pattern.
- **The install vehicle and its cap.** [[jump-serve-progression]] is the drill that carries this, and at 14s-15s you run **stages 1 through 3 only** (standing float, one-step float, two-step jump-float). Stage 4 is jump-topspin and is 16+ material in this program; do not let a servable athlete skip ahead into it. Use the drill's own gates rather than eyeballing the toss: **10 consecutive tosses with a consistent release point** before a stage is attempted, and **7 of 10 to a called zone on two consecutive sets** before a stage is owned and the next one opens [aoc-2024-float-serve-toss].
- **Standing-float retained.** As the workhorse fallback for athletes whose jump-float toss control on the move is still maturing: Mattox's Lift/Step/Swing cue and Dunning's toss-first emphasis ("tossing is the worst skill in volleyball", drill it in isolation) [aoc-2025-float-serve-3-keys][aoc-2024-float-serve-toss].
- **Topspin deferred to 16+.** In this program the women's-game build runs float-dominant through 15s, with topspin entering at 16s for the most advanced servers. The 14s pipeline focuses on jump-float reliability and toss consistency; topspin layering is held until 16s.
- **Ceiling:** jump-topspin remains 16+ for the most advanced servers, kinematic predictors of jump-serve velocity at U-19 show approach velocity alone explaining ~89% of variance, with cocking and CoG management layering in at U-21+ [bari-2023-jump-serve-efficacy-kinematics]. Hold the jump-topspin install; build the approach foundation first.
- **Accountability framework:** Munciana's "aim small, miss small" philosophy applies from day one, every serve targets a finite area (down to a 2-inch noodle target or a single floor box) so athletes hold themselves accountable to placement [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Failure mode:** toss-first failure, athlete spends all the rep load on the contact and tosses inconsistently; reverse to drilling the toss in isolation, no contact, until she puts 10 consecutive tosses through the same release point [aoc-2024-float-serve-toss].
- See [[serving-cues]].

### Defense
- **Body-positioning anchor:** Hips-under-ball for hard-driven attacks (Stone framework) [aoc-2025-controlling-the-dig].
- **Read diagnostic (Josephson's three beats, defined once here).** *Look* is the teachable part: where the eyes go, which is the hitter's approach angle, hips, shoulder line, and hand. *See* is the interpretation: what those cues tell you the attacker intends, which the coach draws out by asking "what did you see?" and then answering it for her. *Decide* is the response the read buys: where to move and what platform to bring. Compartmentalising the three is what lets you diagnose which beat actually failed [aoc-2025-training-reading-look-see-decide]. See [[defense-reading]].
- **B-S-B-H eye sequence, introduced at 14s.** Ball to setter, back to ball, then hitter: the four handoffs the eyes make on every rally [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill]. At 14s you introduce it verbally and coach it in drills; being able to *verbalize* the sequence unprompted is the 15s tryout standard, not the 14s one. See [[defense-reading]].
- **Coaching shortcut:** Munciana's Three P's (posture, position, passion), which is Lingenfelter's own phrasing inside Seat-to-Seam [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. The program gloss "get back, get low, get a touch" is the version said out loud in the gym, in that order, and covers the same three priorities as a ready-position and pursuit shortcut.
- **Perimeter-base stagger:** outside foot high, meaning the sideline foot ahead of the in-court foot so the belly angles back into the court and the defender can seal the line. Lingenfelter's note that this "doesn't happen by accident, it has to be taught" is about this back-court stance, not about blocking [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. See [[defense-perimeter]].
- **Late-14s read milestone:** athlete recognizes shot-vs-swing in the hitter's approach and adjusts depth pre-contact, not post-contact [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill].
- **Failure mode:** platform formed early instead of late on the dig contact.
- See [[defense-cues]].

### Transition
- **Block-to-approach recovery footwork installed:** defaulting to step-back, not run-back, per the landing-mechanics caveat above [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus].
- **Dig-to-approach footwork:** live for OHs and opposites.
- **Out-of-system framing:** Munciana's "you're going to be out of system about 80 percent of the time" framing is age-appropriate at 14s [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **OOS training block: the five-and-five target set.** The Munciana out-of-system protocol is a target, not a slogan: on a bad ball the second contact goes up **five feet off the net and five feet in from the sideline**, so the hitter knows where the ball will be before she starts her approach and the setter is never forced to over-set tight [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. The paired standard is coverage: every covering player puts her **hands on the floor before the ball is caught**, and late hands mean either the cover was slow or the set was not high enough [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Both are installable at 14s and both are checkable from the sideline without a stat sheet. Drill: [[munciana-five-and-five-setting]]. The wider decision-rule playbook (who calls it, who takes the second ball, how the hitters adjust) is at [[transition-out-of-system]].
- **OOS training block, broader:** the setting-and-hitting block trains non-setter setters, dig-then-set patterns, and team out-of-system flows on the assumption that a perfect first-contact pass is the exception, not the rule.
- **Failure mode:** panic-set with hands when a tight pass arrives instead of hopping to a forearm-set or bumping to a teammate. See [[transition-out-of-system]] for the second-contact decision rules that replace the panic.
- See [[transition-cues]].

## Systems repertoire

**[[offense-5-1]] is the expected default at top-club 14s** with a clearly-dominant setter playing all six rotations, three front-row attackers when the setter is back-row, two when she's front-row [aoc-2022-4-2-system].

**"Clearly-dominant setter" is an outcome, not an impression.** She is the athlete who wins the setter competition across *all* your tryout blocks on three named things: hands cleanliness (hand-hinge with side-of-ball contact, no chest-trap, holding up under jump-set tempo), out-of-system bailouts (she gets a hittable five-and-five ball up off a bad pass more often than the alternative does), and match-tempo decisions (she picks the right hitter at speed, not just the safe one). If one athlete takes all three, run the 5-1. **If two setters split those three, you do not have a clearly-dominant setter and you run the 6-2.** Splitting them is the signal, not a reason to argue about it.

**Hybrid 5-1 / 6-2 mechanics.** The hybrid keeps the 5-1 as the season default and drops into 6-2 only in the rotations where the primary setter is a genuine liability at the net or in the back court, substituting the second setter for those rotations and returning to the 5-1 after [aoc-2022-hybrid-5-1-two-setters]. Mattox's specialty split is the cleanest version of the same idea: the taller setter plays her front-row rotations for the blocking presence, the quicker setter plays back row for the defense, and the swap is drilled as a rehearsed substitution rather than an in-match improvisation [aoc-2022-hybrid-5-1-two-setters]. Full mechanics on both sides at [[offense-5-1]] and [[offense-6-2]].

**[[offense-6-2]] is the fallback** for rosters without a clearly-dominant setter, two setters rotating through, three front-row attackers per rotation, manageable rotation tracking; carried over from the 12s-13s install [aoc-2021-teach-rotations-beginners]. [[offense-4-2]] is **absent at 14s** as a year-end shape, the 4-2 is the 11s entry-level offense and a transitional 12s install fallback; by 14s rotation tracking should be solid enough to support 6-2 minimum [aoc-2022-4-2-system]. [[offense-6-6]] is appropriate only for true-beginner 14s where rotating through every position is the developmental point [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules]; nationally-ranked club 14s have moved past it.

Serve-receive: [[serve-receive-4-player]] is the default; **[[serve-receive-3-player]] is introduced late-14s** once passing identity has settled into the libero-and-two-OHs core. That is the first appearance of a three-passer look in the program ladder, matching what [[13s]] defers to this year. [[serve-receive-libero-split]] does not arrive until 15s and is not a 14s install. Defense: [[defense-perimeter]] is primary for new-to-team rosters; **[[defense-middle-middle]] is the program-identity default at top-club 14s** (libero-at-LB, MB anchor at 6m middle, cross-shading executable, libero-deep adjustment drilled as scoutable opponent-specific shift) [gms-nd-middle-middle-defense][gms-nd-libero-in-volleyball][aoc-2015-hagglund-libero-mb-lb-positioning]; [[defense-rotation]] introduced as a concept only. Blocking: [[commit-blocking]] is the primary read scheme because the decision is simpler (commit to the middle on every play); [[read-blocking]] is introduced as a concept, not as the match-default scheme. Bunch-read concept is introducible at top-club 14s as a tactical layer (three blockers stacked tight, reading the setter's hand release before committing) [aoc-2026-bunch-read-blocking]; bunch-read as the default scheme with full-decision-tree variants stays an [[age-lens-14u]] ceiling: HS+ material [aoc-2023-swing-blocking-when].

## Athletic profile

[[age-lens-14u]] carries the full treatment of the 14U physical picture, and this section deliberately does not repeat it. What follows is the 14s-specific residue plus one pointer per theme.

**Three things that are specifically a 14s problem.**

- **The platform is on a moving target.** Growth at this age is rapid and asymmetric, so a passer whose platform was grooved in October can be genuinely worse in February through no fault of her own. Budget blocked-rep time mid-season to revisit the platform install for any athlete who grew two or more inches over the holidays; treat it as maintenance, not as a regression.
- **The CSCS owns programming; you own movement quality and rep-count discipline.** That boundary is firmer at 14s than at any other age because this is where parents start asking for vertical-jump programs. Defer the load question and keep the landing question. See [[physical]].
- **Approach velocity is a technical problem, not a strength problem.** Women approach at slower speeds and use shorter penultimate steps than men in matched-elite samples, and strength and power do not fully explain the gap [fuchs-2019-male-female-spike-biomechanics]. That makes approach velocity, penultimate-step length, and plant angle coachable from cue at 14s rather than gated behind the weight room. Vertical-jump numbers are not a coachable end-point here; these are.

**Everything else routes to the lens.** Specialization as an injury-prevention lever, including the Post-2019 finding that volleyball was the only sport in a matched youth comparison where specialization significantly raised overuse risk [post-2019-specialization-overuse-volleyball]. See [[age-lens-14u]]. ACL and landing mechanics, including the neuromuscular-training dose and the position-specific incidence in adolescent female outside hitters [sassi-2025-acl-volleyball-systematic-review][zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus]. See [[age-lens-14u]]. Silent overuse in knee and shoulder, and why "no pain yet" does not mean "no problem yet" [gisslen-2005-jumpers-knee-elite-junior-volleyball][kugler-1996-shoulder-pain-volleyball-attackers]. See [[age-lens-14u]]. RED-S recognition and the referral path to a physician and registered dietitian [coelho-2021-female-athlete-triad-reds]. See [[age-lens-14u]] and [[physical]].

## Mental & coachability markers

A late-14s athlete who is HS-tryout-ready demonstrates four mental markers in matches and in practice:

1. **Self-correcting after a clear coach-cue.** The next rep shows visible re-anchoring. Silent compliance and defensive reactivity both fall short of it.
2. **Consistent effort independent of score.** Munciana's "every drill has a winner and a loser" culture only works when athletes don't cash out at 14-22 in the wash drill [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing]; at 14s this should be installed even when fragile.
3. **Communication during plays.** Calling balls, calling seams, calling switches in real time.
4. **Visible engagement during teammate reps.** Watching, not phone-checking, between drill turns.

**Self-talk is a sustained block, not a one-shot tool.** For 14s-and-older athletes who can engage with sport-psychology work, 8-week self-talk interventions reduced somatic anxiety, increased self-confidence, improved general self-efficacy with sustained gains, and produced measurably better coach-rated performance in junior sub-elite athletes; 1-week interventions produced only transient effects [walter-2019-self-talk-junior-athletes]. The implication: integrate self-talk practice into the season-long routine. **Self-feedback capability** is itself a developmental milestone with a concrete age: Munciana names this explicitly as a marker that distinguishes older from younger teams [aoc-2025-cc-skill-training-clubwide].

## Promotion criteria

These promotion criteria are the contract for moving from 14s to 15s and the input scoring criteria for [[tryout-rubric-14u]]. Each criterion is observable in a 60-90-minute tryout session and is graded on a 1-5 scale (1: does not demonstrate; 3: consistent at 14s; 5: 15s-ready).

- **Passing:** **≥60% in-system** from a moderate-pace standing-float serve, where *in-system* means a **pass graded 2 or better** on the 0-3 scale defined at [[passing-serve-receive]] [aoc-2020-dean-passing-stats]. The geometry that makes a 2 a 2: the ball lands in the setter zone, a **six-foot-wide box centred on the setter's base at the net**, and peaks at **seven feet or higher** so she can play it standing up. Below seven feet the setter is reaching and the ball is a 1 no matter where it lands. The 14s bar reads the same as the 13s bar on paper, but the serve is faster and the box is smaller, so it is a harder standard than it looks. Quiet platform under jam-balls with the hop-back adjustment, not the chest-reach [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing]. Overhead-pass decision is deliberate. Pass-and-cover habit visible, sprints to coverage spot after contact, not still admiring the pass [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing]. Promotes to 15s if **3 across all four sub-criteria**.
- **Setting:** For setter candidates only, hand-hinge mechanics with side-of-ball contact, no chest-trap [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge]; outside set delivers a hittable ball from in-system pass; backset competent without telegraphing; understands and can demonstrate Tempo 3 footwork on tossed balls (Tempo 2 nice-to-have, Tempo 1 not required) [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-tempo]. Promotes if **3 across hand-hinge / outside set / backset / Tempo 3 demo**.
- **Hitting:** Three-step approach with visible "start slow, accelerate the last two" tempo [aoc-2024-attack-approach]; arm swing with elbow-lead and high-hand contact, not a flat sidearm [aoc-2024-attack-arm-mechanics]; in-shot-selection inventory of at least two of {big cross, line, tip}. Female-specific marker: penultimate-step length and approach velocity coachable from cue, not strength-bound [fuchs-2019-male-female-spike-biomechanics]. Promotes if **3 across approach / arm swing / shot selection**.
- **Blocking:** Static-and-shuffle footwork reliable for pin blockers, with the jump-add explicitly *not* expected of them this year; full swing-block execution (footwork + jump) emerging for middles at top-club 14s [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners][aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking]; outside-foot-forward closing position (program blocking convention); hand-press with net penetration; default landing pattern is stick or step-back, not run-back [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus]. Promotes if **3 across footwork (full swing-block for middles, footwork-only for pins) / hand position / landing pattern**.
- **Serving:** Jump-float in rotation (toss consistency is the gating criterion, not contact) [aoc-2024-float-serve-toss]. Standing-float retained as the in-zone fallback (≥60% in target zone). Aim-small target accountability: athlete can name a specific spot before each serve rather than a general area [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Promotes if **3 across jump-float in rotation, standing-float fallback, target accountability**.
- **Defense:** Three P's (posture, position, passion) [munciana-2022-camp-drills], visible through the program gloss "get back, get low, get a touch" as a habit, not as a coach-prompted reset; Look/See/Decide read on shot-vs-swing pre-contact [aoc-2025-training-reading-look-see-decide]; platform formation late, not early [aoc-2025-controlling-the-dig]. Promotes if **3 across three P's / read / late platform**.
- **Transition:** Block-to-approach footwork (step-back default), dig-to-approach footwork for OHs and opposites; recognizable out-of-system protocol, does not panic-set with hands when a tight pass arrives, hops to a forearm-set or bumps to a teammate, and gets the bail ball to the five-and-five target rather than tight to the net [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Cover arrives with hands on the floor before the ball is caught [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. See [[transition-out-of-system]] for the full decision-rule set. Promotes if **3 across recovery footwork and OOS protocol**.
- **Mental & coachability:** Self-corrects after one cue (not three); consistent effort across the score; communicates in-rally; engages during teammate reps. **This is non-negotiable**: a player below 3 on coachability does not promote, regardless of skill scores.

## Season operations

Program convention, not sourced guidance.

**Checkpoints.** Re-score the whole roster on the same [[tryout-rubric-14u]] rows at week 6 and week 12. Same rows, same anchors, same evaluators where you can manage it. You are measuring the delta, so changing the instrument mid-season throws the measurement away.

**Behind-standard protocol.** For any athlete under the age-appropriate column, name her lowest two criteria, assign a specific extra-rep block from [[14s-drills]] against each one, and re-check those two rows in two weeks. Two cycles with no movement is the escalation trigger: change the intervention rather than repeating it, and pull in the position coach or director.

**Error-correction priority order.** When several things are wrong at once: feet before platform, toss before swing, approach before arm swing, landing before contact height. In each pair the second item is usually a symptom of the first, and fixing it first just installs a compensation on top of the real problem.

## Common coaching corrections

- **Reach-and-pop on the platform.** Athlete steps into the ball with arms first; coach reverses to "stopped and balanced first, platform second", Kiraly's first key [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass][aoc-2023-passing-shuffle-not-reach]. Drill: [[munciana-straight-line-passing]] for the closure-on-balance reset.
- **Chest-trap on the overhead set.** Setter contacts low and pushes through with the chest; coach reverses to hand-hinge with side-of-ball contact [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge]. Drill: [[target-setting]] with hands-only constraint.
- **Penultimate-step too long, plant too far from the ball.** OH approaches into the net rather than parallel to it; coach reverses to "start slow, accelerate the last two" with the penultimate-step shortening implied [aoc-2024-attack-approach][fuchs-2019-male-female-spike-biomechanics]. Drill: [[approach-and-swing]] with a tape line marking the plant.
- **Wrist-snap as the cue for hitting.** Over-emphasizes wrist; modern teaching uses arm-and-hand dynamic with high-hand torque [aoc-2024-attack-arm-mechanics]. Reverses to "high hand, lead with the elbow", Mattox's keywords. Anti-cue: do not say "snap your wrist." See [[hitting-cues]].
- **Run-back landing after a block.** Highest knee-valgus moment landing pattern, never the default [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus]. Coach reverses to step-back-and-recover, not sprint-back. Hard rule: any athlete with prior knee-pain history practices stick-only landings until cleared.
- **Toss-first failure on the float serve.** Athlete spends all the rep load on the contact and tosses inconsistently. Reverses to drilling the toss in isolation, no contact, until the toss lands repeatably in the same spot [aoc-2024-float-serve-toss]. Drill: any of the [[munciana-5-6-seam-noodle]] / [[munciana-5-6-seam-strike]] target series.
- **Standing in line during reps.** Munciana's design: "we'll touch more balls in a five-minute period than some people will pass in an hour" [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Reverses to short-window drills with rapid scoring resets and no standing lines [usav-2009-cap-game-like-training].
- **Verbal cueing at the wrong granularity.** Moran's youth replication of the OPTIMAL-theory cue paradigm found *no significant differences* between external, internal, analogy, and neutral-control cues on whole-body explosive tasks, and neutral-control was actually superior to internal cueing on vertical jump [moran-2023-verbal-coaching-cues-youth]. The "always external" rule is over-stated for youth. External focus stays defensible for **arm-swing-specific** cues where 3-D motion-capture data on young female spikers does support an external-focus advantage on arm-swing velocity [slovak-2023-external-focus-volleyball-spike]: but coach to the player, observe, and retain what works for that athlete.

## Recommended drills

Drill pick-list with full age-context: see [[14s-drills]] for the complete catalog. Backbone drills below. The 46-drill [[munciana-2022-camp-drills]] library is heavily age-band-versatile and works as the operational spine of a 14s practice arc:

- **Warm-up:** [[dynamic-warmup-volleyball]], [[jump-warmup]], [[munciana-two-man-shuffle]] (pursuit footwork as the first thing athletes do, no ball) [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Passing:** [[butterfly-passing]], [[munciana-three-man-tilt]], [[munciana-rush-and-push]], [[munciana-speed-close]], [[munciana-straight-line-passing]], [[munciana-directional-pass]], [[munciana-triangle-passing-box-b]] for triangle geometry [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Setting:** [[target-setting]], [[three-setter-rotation]], [[front-back-sets]], [[munciana-tight-triangles]] for setting/hitting integration.
- **Hitting:** [[approach-and-swing]], [[pass-set-hit]] with target zones.
- **Blocking:** [[block-footwork-ladder]] carries Rosen's 10-foot-line footwork progression with no jump; for pin blockers that is the whole year's blocking install [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners]. [[block-touch-drill]] for hand position, run at its 14U adaptation where the win is hands above the tape on every rep. [[commit-block-trigger]] for the commit-block decision, simplified to a single read. [[swing-block-shuffle]] for top-club middles only, and only after the jump-add is reliable; it is an HS-tagged drill and it stays an extension block, not a roster-wide rep.
- **Serving:** [[serve-targets]], [[zone-serving]], [[jump-serve-progression]] (stages 1-3 only at 14s; stage 4 is jump-topspin and belongs at 16+), [[munciana-5-6-seam-noodle]], [[munciana-5-6-seam-strike]], [[munciana-no-gut-serving]], [[munciana-five-before-ten]] for aggressive-mentality target work [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Defense:** [[munciana-speedball]] (the intro to defense), [[munciana-three-way-release]], [[munciana-mountain-d]], [[munciana-seat-to-seam]], [[munciana-pursuit-attack]] for movement-first defensive identity [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Competitive 6v6:** [[munciana-biggie-smalls]] (3-in-a-row), [[munciana-prove-it]] (must-serve-and-score), [[munciana-momentum]] (escalating point values), [[munciana-tug-of-war]], [[munciana-finish-strong]] (19-19 reset on miss): the chassis varies, the technique stays constant; the principle is that competitive 6v6 trains psychological habits that show up in real matches [munciana-2022-camp-drills].

**Practice-design note** (Munciana operational depth): drill volume in tight windows is a stated design principle, drills are short, scoring resets fast, athletes accumulate reps inside small geometric formats (triangles, tilt-chains, two-ball patterns) rather than long lines [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Always run left and right, symmetry is the default, not an option [munciana-2022-camp-drills].

Four numbers from the same tape make that principle plannable [munciana-2022-camp-drills]:

- **45-second increments are the default rep window.** Most drills in the library are timed at 45 seconds, not counted in reps. Write the practice plan in windows, not in sets.
- **25 quality passes with setter scoring is the drill target format.** The count belongs to the pair or the group, the setter judges quality, and the drill ends when the number lands rather than when the clock does. Run it left, then run it right.
- **Five-minute serving blocks bracket every water break.** Serving always ends a focus section: serve, water, serve. Almost every serving drill in the library runs five minutes.
- **Cooperative and competitive are the two drill kinds, and the coach names which one is running.** Competitive drills put athletes against each other; cooperative drills put them against the clock or against a number ("they compete against your watch, they compete against a number"). Both are accountable; only one is adversarial, and at 14s you need both in the same practice. **Differential-learning schedules** (every-rep-different) significantly out-performed both repetitive and passive-control groups across underhand pass / overhand pass / overhand serve in matched adolescent female absolute-beginners [apidogo-2021-differential-learning-volleyball]. That supports noise-injection variability for 14s skill sessions. **Implicit-then-explicit progression** for serve decision-making: front-load perception-based reps before layering on the verbal "rules of where to serve" [lola-2012-implicit-explicit-volleyball-serving].

## Practice ratio across the season

The blocked / small-group / 6v6 mix for 14s runs **40/35/25 → 30/35/35 → 25/35/40 → 20/30/50 → 30/30/40** across preseason, mid-season, late-season, pre-tournament-peak, and taper respectively. The 14s-specific shape: heavy pattern install through the platform and hand-hinge install window [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge]; the small-group bucket holds steady at 30-35% year-round as the methodologically-richest practice density [shea-1979-contextual-interference]; bucket 1 dips to 20% at peak and bumps back to 30% at taper for polish-rep contact freshness without fatigue. The growth-spurt reality means the platform is on a moving target, revisit blocked install reps mid-season for athletes who grew 2+ inches over the holidays. Drill-format anchors: [[butterfly-passing]], [[pass-set-hit]], [[serve-targets]] (blocked); [[queen-of-the-court]], [[wash-drill]], [[cooperative-25-goal]] (small-group); [[gold-medal-scrimmage]], [[transition-rally]] (6v6). See [[practice-ratios]] for the full matrix and adjacent-age-band trajectories.

## Recommended cues

Cue dictionaries (to be filled in alongside this guide): [[passing-cues]], [[setting-cues]], [[hitting-cues]], [[blocking-cues]], [[serving-cues]], [[defense-cues]], [[transition-cues]]. Anchor cues for 14s, by skill, see [[mike-lingenfelter]] for the Munciana voice and [[karch-kiraly]] for the USA-CAP voice:

- **Passing:** Kiraly's 4 keys, stopped & balanced / thumbs together / set the angle / simple one-way motion [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass]. Lingenfelter quiet-platform / pass-and-cover [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing].
- **Setting:** Hand-hinge / side-of-ball contact / vision-on-target [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge]. Lingenfelter Tempo 1/2/3 by left-foot plant timing [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-middle-tempo].
- **Hitting:** "Start slow, accelerate the last two" (Schmidt) [aoc-2024-attack-approach]. Sight-90 / Elbow-Lead / Lift-and-Whip (Mattox) [aoc-2021-teach-spiking]. High hand, torque (MacDonald) [aoc-2024-attack-arm-mechanics].
- **Blocking:** "Outside foot forward on the close," the pin-blocker closing stance: program blocking convention rather than a sourced Munciana blocking cue (the tape carries no blocking instruction). Read-then-jump. Penetrate-not-cover.
- **Serving:** Aim-small-miss-small (Munciana) [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Lift/Step/Swing (Mattox). Toss-first ("tossing is the worst skill in volleyball", Dunning) [aoc-2024-float-serve-toss]. Aggressive-mentality framing (Munciana five-before-ten) [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Defense:** Three P's (posture, position, passion, in Lingenfelter's words), glossed in the gym as "get back, get low, get a touch" [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. "Outside foot high," plus "this doesn't happen by accident, it has to be taught," the perimeter-base stagger for back-court defenders (Munciana) [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Hips-under-ball (Stone) [aoc-2025-controlling-the-dig]. Look/See/Decide (Josephson) [aoc-2025-training-reading-look-see-decide]. Read the hitter, not the ball [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill].
- **Transition:** Block-to-approach (step-back default, never run-back as default) [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus]. "Five off, five in," the Munciana out-of-system target set [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. "Hands on the floor before the ball is caught," the cover standard [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Detect / call / bail-to-pin is the program's OOS sequence; see [[transition-out-of-system]] for the decision rules behind it.
