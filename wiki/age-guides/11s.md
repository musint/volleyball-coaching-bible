# Age Guide: 11s

## Overview & development phase

The 11U athlete is at the **introduction phase transitioning toward fundamentals** — past the movement-discovery window of ages 3–9 [usav-2026-youth-volleyball-tips], not yet at the durable skill-acquisition stage where 12U technique milestones become non-negotiable [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals]. For most clubs 11U is the bridge year: a player who was only loosely organized at [[10s]] starts forming a recognizable platform, attempts an overhand serve for the first time, and meets a real 6v6 entry-level offense. Calendar-age-wise the 11U athlete is typically 10–11 by USAV calendar age, pre-pubertal through early puberty.

Two USAV instruments shape this phase. **Simplified Youth Rules** still nominally cover ages 3–11 — the 11U athlete is in USAV's last codified small-sided window, where 2v2 / 3v3 / 4v4, lightweight youth balls, and unlimited substitution remain the official defaults [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules].

**Net height.** USAV's youth band runs **5-6 feet for the youngest players, rising to 7 feet by 12U** [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules]; 11s therefore sits at or under that 7-foot ceiling. This program plays 11s at **7'0"** [unsourced], the same height it plays [[10s]] and [[12s]], so that nothing about the net changes underneath an athlete during the three years her swing is being built.

**Growing Kids' Volleyball** carries the 12U-and-under philosophy that engagement, pair-based practice, and over-the-net-first principles drive retention [usav-2026-growing-kids-volleyball]. A competitive 11U program straddles both: USAV's small-sided defaults remain the practice-design baseline, while **6v6 elements are introduced progressively** so a player can graduate to [[12s]] with rotations recognized, an overhand serve rehearsed, and a real platform under construction.

The "introduction" label means: technique and systems are *introduced*, not mastered. Ingrained mechanical errors at 11U are costly to retrain later, so the coach's job is to install good shapes (platform, hand triangle, approach footwork) before high-volume reps cement bad ones [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals]. The recency-leaning operating model on this wiki [[age-lens-14u]] applies in compressed form: modern coaching language (Kiraly's 4 keys, Munciana's pursuit-and-closure framing, Mattox's accelerate-the-last-two-steps) lands more cleanly on a beginner than legacy phrasing. The Munciana club-wide-alignment principle — an 11U player already speaks the same vocabulary an 18U player speaks [aoc-2025-cc-skill-training-clubwide] — implies serious programs install the *right* cues at 11U, not the *easy* ones.

## Skill milestones

### Passing

- **Platform shape:** thumbs together, wrists straight, arms slightly off the chest, knees bent, shoulders over the front foot — recognizable on every contact, even off-center [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals].
- **Kiraly's 4 keys frame the cognitive load:** stopped and balanced before contact, thumbs together, **set the angle with the shoulders and hips**, simple one-way motion [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass].
- **Say it that way, not the other way.** The angle to target comes from rotating the body so a flat platform faces the target, **never by tilting an arm**. "Set the angle with your platform" is listed as an anti-cue in [[passing-cues]] precisely because it produces an asymmetric platform with a gap between the forearms. At 11s the shortest working form is **"aim with your shoulders."**
- **Highest-yield 11U focus:** "stopped and balanced" — beginners reach instead of move; Munciana opens its drill library with a no-ball pursuit shuffle for exactly this reason [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Milestones:**
  - Pass a tossed or slow-served ball to setter target with consistent arm shape.
  - "Shuffle, don't reach" footwork on balls outside the body.
  - Hold the platform finish a beat after contact.
  - Recognize overhead-pass territory vs. forearm territory (concept only).
- **See:** [[passing-cues]], [[passing-forearm]].

### Setting

- **Default progression:** catch-and-set for most athletes; a small number of taller / coordinated kids ready for live setting by season's end [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals].
- **Hand shape (same one elite setters use):** triangle above the forehead, thumbs not touching, elbows slightly forward, feet shoulder-width with right foot forward, knees bent [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals].
- **Hand-hinge:** wrist cocks back and snaps forward as legs extend — the day-one setter skill in modern AOC coaching [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge].
- **Milestones:**
  - Catch-and-set 10 reps with hand triangle and no thumb-collision.
  - For setter-track candidates: live-set a tossed ball to target so **the ball peaks 10-15 feet above the floor** on 6 of 10 attempts. This is the same floor-referenced number used at [[10s]] and on [[tryout-rubric-10s]]; measure from the floor, not the net, because 11s net heights vary by gym. Nelson's underlying rule is "ready to transition from passing to setting" [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals].
  - Left-to-right shuffle footwork.
  - Legs-extend-up power source, not "all wrist."
- **Reality check:** most 11U athletes will not be cleanly live-setting 5–10 feet by season end; ingraining the right hand shape in catch-and-set reps counts.
- **See:** [[setting-cues]], [[setting-hands]].

### Hitting

- **Three-stage progression (Nelson):** down-ball no-jump → 2-step modified approach → 3-step introduction late season for advanced athletes [aoc-2024-kids-attacking-fundamentals].
- **Workhorse stance:** 2-step "Flamingo" [aoc-2024-kids-attacking-fundamentals].
- **Tempo cue (Schmidt):** start slow, accelerate the last two steps [aoc-2024-attack-approach].
- **Milestones:**
  - Down-ball arm swing from puppet position (elbow up, hand high, contact in front).
  - 2-step approach with isolated jump on accurate toss.
  - 3-step approach attempted by season's end where coordination supports it.
  - Contact at peak of jump (concept, not consistent execution).
- **See:** [[hitting-cues]], [[hitting-approach]], [[hitting-arm-swing]].

### Blocking

- **Scope:** single-blocker concept under USAV simplified rules [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules].
- **Skill focus:** hand-on-the-net ready position and a controlled stationary jump — no swing-blocking footwork yet.
- **Reality:** most 11U attacks are free balls and down-balls; blocking practice is more about **net awareness and not getting injured** than stopping attacks.
- **Milestones:**
  - **Hands reach at or above the tape at the top of a controlled stationary jump**, thumbs up, fingers spread. Pre-jump, an 11-year-old's hands are nowhere near the tape and never will be. The standard is what the hands do at the peak of the jump, not what they do standing on the floor.
  - Balanced pre-jump posture: hands at eye level, feet shoulder-width, weight on the balls of the feet.
  - Stationary 2-foot jump straight up without net contact.
  - Recognize when to block vs. back up to defense.
- **See:** [[blocking-cues]], [[blocking-footwork]], [[blocking-hand-position]].

### Serving

- **The 11U headline:** the year the overhand serve is introduced — at 10U underhand is default and overhand is exploratory [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules]; at 12U the standing float should be the default [aoc-2024-kids-serving-fundamentals]; 11U is the bridge.
- **Nelson's five-serve taxonomy at this age:** static floater is the workhorse; Japanese floater (rewards wrist control) introduced as a concept only [aoc-2024-kids-serving-fundamentals]. Topspin is deferred to 16+ — the float is the right women's-game build for the youth pipeline.
- **Entry sequence:** Newkirk's "Deliver and Pop" — reach + drop + pop [aoc-2021-youth-15-drills].

**The 11s serving-line progression (one published progression; [[tryout-rubric-11s]] and [[11s-drills]] reference this, they do not restate it).**

1. **Install off the floor first.** Kneel, then single-leg, roughly 3 m off the net, serving to a partner-catcher [[munciana-kneel-single-leg-serve]]. The goal is a real swing with clean contact. Distance is irrelevant here and depth is not coached at all.
2. **Stand up at the attack line.** Once the swing is clean off the knee, the athlete stands and serves overhand **from the attack line**. Not the end line. An 11-year-old put on the end line before her swing works will arm-throw the ball to reach it, and that pattern costs two seasons to unlearn.
3. **Back up in steps, gated on contact quality.** Move her back roughly 1 m per session **only while contact stays clean**. The moment the swing degrades into a throw or a lob, she goes back to the previous line for the rest of the session. Distance is earned, never assigned by calendar.
4. **End-of-season target:** **2-3 feet inside the end line for most athletes; the end line for advanced athletes.** An athlete who finishes the season serving cleanly from the attack line has still met the 11s milestone: the milestone is the swing, not the distance.

- **Milestones:**
  - Underhand serve consistently clears the net from the end line (10U baseline preserved).
  - **Overhand serve with successful contact on 3 of 10 attempts** from her current line in the progression above. See the definition of "successful contact" under `## Promotion criteria`.
  - Toss-first emphasis: consistent toss precedes any swing coaching. Measured as **8 of 10 tosses landing inside a 2-foot box in front of the hitting shoulder**, swing withheld.
  - Aggressive intent — **serve at a target**, not just over the net [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. Scored as a landing outcome: ball lands in the correct half of the receiving court on 4 of 10 in-court serves.
- **See:** [[serving-cues]], [[serving-float]].

### Defense

- **Operating mode:** read-and-pursue more than platform precision.
- **Munciana's Three P's: Posture, Position, Passion.** Those are Lingenfelter's own words for the three P's of defense [munciana-2022-camp-drills]. The **program gloss** is **"get back, get low, get a touch,"** always in that word order, and it is what you actually say on the court to an 11-year-old. Keep the two labelled: the P's are the source, the gloss is ours.
- **Platform mechanics transfer from passing;** the new content is the **read**.
- **Reading is not a separate drill (Kiraly):** "reading is not separate from skill; it is part of every skill" — embed reading questions ("where is the setter looking?") into every defensive rep rather than running isolated reading drills [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill].
- **Milestones:**
  - Ready position (knees bent, weight forward, hands in front).
  - Pursue with a recovery touch (not a clean pass — a touch that keeps the rally alive).
  - Recognize free balls vs. attacks (concept).
- **See:** [[defense-cues]], [[defense-platform]], [[defense-reading]].

### Transition

- **Single 11U concept:** after a contact, get back to your ready spot.
- **Out of scope:** block-to-attack and defense-to-attack patterns (not yet executable).
- **Unit-level skill:** pass-set-hit as a *shape* — passer to setter, setter to hitter, hitter swings — with every player attempting all three contacts in 6v6 cooperative play.
- **Milestones:**
  - Recover to ready position after every contact.
  - Pass-set-hit shape executed cooperatively (low success rate is fine; the shape is the point).
  - Urgency awareness (concept).
- **See:** [[transition-cues]].

## Systems repertoire

- **Offense:**
  - [[offense-6-6]] (every player rotates through every position) remains the developmentally-correct practice default for 11U athletes still figuring out where they belong [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules].
  - **Simple [[offense-4-2]] is the entry-level competitive offense** — front-row setter in all six rotations, two designated setters opposite each other, three front-row attackers [aoc-2022-4-2-system].
  - Mattox's framing applies most strongly at 11U: front-row setter is always the same, the two setters are interchangeable, rotation tracking is minimal — "easier for less experienced players to learn than 5-1 or 6-2" [aoc-2022-4-2-system].
  - **Out of scope at 11U:** the Slide and back-row Bic that Mattox cites as 4-2 weapons at HS+ levels — timing too tight, approach too complex.
  - **The 11U 4-2 is the *simple* 4-2:** setter sets, front-row OH or middle hits.
  - **Ceiling:** simple 4-2 entry by season's end. [[offense-6-2]] and [[offense-5-1]] explicitly out of scope [aoc-2021-teach-rotations-beginners][aoc-2022-4-2-system].
- **Serve receive:** **4-passer or 5-passer U or W shape.** Five passers is the honest 11s default (everybody receives, nobody hides), and the 4-passer U is the step toward [[serve-receive-4-player]] that 12s installs. **Three-passer formations are late-14s+ material program-wide** and have no place at 11s: they concentrate court on athletes who do not yet have the range or the read to cover it. Overlap rules are taught conceptually [aoc-2021-teach-rotations-beginners] — front-row players ahead of back-row partners, back-row players behind their front-row partners, server "not on the court" [aoc-2021-teach-rotations-beginners].
- **Defense:** [[defense-perimeter]] is the only viable shape — each athlete owns a zone, simple geometry, low reading load. **Defensive-identity install (top-club program):** [[defense-middle-middle]] is introducible as a *concept* (the 6m mid-court anchor, plus **the middle-back-deep pattern that later becomes libero-at-LB**) by season's end for rosters that have stabilized perimeter. There is no libero at 11s and no libero position to assign — what you are installing is the shape, so that when a libero exists at 13s/14s she inherits a pattern the team already knows. Cross-shading and read-and-cut elements stay 12s+ material [gms-nd-middle-middle-defense].
- **Blocking:** single-blocker only per USAV simplified rules [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules]; [[commit-blocking]] as the conceptual framing.
- **Positions:** no specialization at 11U.
  - Some athletes trend toward setter, hitter, or libero profiles by season's end and the coach **notices** signals — but assigning a position is premature [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules][aoc-2025-cc-skill-training-clubwide].
  - Rotate every athlete through every position across the season.

## Athletic profile

11U is mostly pre-pubertal with some early-puberty athletes. Growth is uneven; coordination shifts underneath the player. Jump-training and plyometrics are a **CSCS conversation, not a coach conversation** — see [[physical]].

- **Movement quality first:** multi-directional ready-position footwork (shuffle, drop step, cross-over) drilled in warm-ups; jumping mechanics taught for safe landing more than vertical gain.
- **Multi-sport encouragement (evidence-backed, not philosophical):** Post et al. (2019) is unambiguous — high volleyball specialization at the youth level produces OR ~2.3 for overuse injury, year-round (>8 months/year) OR ~2.0, with 45.7% of youth volleyball athletes reporting prior overuse injury (highest among three sports studied) [post-2019-specialization-overuse-volleyball]. Multi-sport play at 11U is injury prevention.
- **Load management literacy for parents:** most 11U overuse comes from accumulated practice load across sports; the coach's lever is communication.
- **No max-effort jump training:** plyometric-heavy conditioning is deferred to a CSCS.

## Mental & coachability markers

11U is the year a player starts processing feedback **with intent to apply it** rather than just nodding. Markers worth observing:

- **Self-correction within a rep cycle.** After feedback, does rep N+1 attempt the correction, or look identical to the prior one? The first is a coachability signal; the second is a "needs more reps" signal, not a deficit.
- **Tolerance for repetition.** Does the athlete accept that platform shape needs many reps, or push to "play games" before fundamentals are stable?
- **Asks questions vs. nods** — asking is a coachability strength at 11U.
- **USAV's 3–11U feedback stance:** **FeedForward** (encouraging reminders about *what to do next* rather than criticism of past errors) is the dominant feedback mode, and "Catch them doing it right" outweighs error-correction [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules].
- **Cue-language evidence is honest about its limits:** a multi-site youth replication of the Wulf external-focus paradigm on jumping and sprinting found **no significant differences** between external, internal, analogy, and neutral-control cues — and *neutral control was actually superior to internal cueing on vertical jump* [moran-2023-verbal-coaching-cues-youth].
- **Implication:** don't over-engineer cue selection. Rep volume and consistent vocabulary outweigh cue-type optimization at this age.
- **Munciana's rep-based confidence principle:** "reps equal confidence" — anxious 11U athletes are usually under-repped, not under-talented [aoc-2025-cc-skill-training-clubwide].

## Promotion criteria

Promotion criteria specify what a player must demonstrate to graduate from 11U to [[12s]]. **The corresponding [[tryout-rubric-11s]] translates these criteria into a 1-5 scoring scale across nine rows**, so the milestones below are the unifying spine of 11U evaluation. Each criterion is observable in a 60-90-minute tryout setting or across season-end practices.

**Every criterion below carries a count, a distance, or a target.** A criterion an evaluator cannot tally is a criterion two evaluators will score differently, and at 11s the whole point of the rubric is that two coaches watching the same athlete arrive at the same number. The numbers below are copied verbatim into the rubric cells; where the two documents ever disagree, this page is the source of truth.

**Passing — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Recognizable platform (thumbs together, wrists straight, knees bent) on **8 of 10 contacts**, including off-center balls.
- Shuffle-not-reach on balls outside the body on **6 of 10** balls fed 3-5 feet to either side at tryout pace.
- Passes a tossed or slow-served ball into the setter zone on **6 of 10** attempts with the platform shape intact.

**Setting — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Hand-triangle shape above forehead with no thumb collision on **8 of 10** catch-and-set reps.
- For setter-track candidates: live-set a tossed ball to a target so **the ball peaks 10-15 feet above the floor** on **6 of 10** attempts [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals].
- Correctly calls "hands" or "platform" on **8 of 10** balls fed alternately above and below the shoulders (the observable form of overhead-vs-forearm recognition).

**Hitting — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Down-ball arm swing from puppet position with elbow-up, contact-in-front shape on **7 of 10** coach-fed balls.
- 2-step approach onto an accurate toss with isolated jump on **5 of 10** attempts.
- **At least 3 attempts** at a 3-step approach across the evaluation (form not yet expected to be clean). Athletes who saw the late-season 3-step introduction at [[10s]] arrive with this already banked.

**Blocking — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- **Hands reach at or above the tape at the top of a controlled stationary jump** on **6 of 10** jumps, thumbs up and fingers spread, with no net contact. Pre-jump hand height is not the standard and never was.
- Correctly chooses block-or-back-up on **6 of 10** coach-called situations.

**Serving — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Reliable underhand serve from the end line: **7 of 10** in-court (the 10U baseline holds at 11U-promotion).
- **Overhand serve with successful contact on 3 of 10 attempts** from her current line in the published progression above. This is the load-bearing 11U-specific milestone: the overhand attempt does not need to score, but it needs to exist as an attempted skill, not a hypothetical one.
- **Successful contact is defined as:** clean hand-on-ball contact with the ball travelling forward over the net from her current designated line. **In or out both count.** A shanked contact, a contact off the wrist or forearm, a ball that fails to cross the net, and a ball tossed and caught all count as failures. Nothing about where the ball lands enters this definition; landing is scored separately.
- Toss consistency: **8 of 10** tosses land inside a 2-foot box in front of the hitting shoulder with the swing withheld.

**Defense — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Ready position held through the opponent's offensive contact on **8 of 10** rallies observed.
- Touches a pursued ball on **5 of 10** balls fed 5-8 feet away (recovery touch counts, and does not need to be a clean dig).
- Correctly calls "free" or "hit" before the ball crosses on **6 of 10** coach-fed balls.

**Transition — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Recovers to her ready spot after contact rather than admiring the ball on **8 of 10** rallies observed.
- Completes her role in a cooperative pass-set-hit shape on **5 of 10** attempts without freezing.

**Mental & coachability — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Attempts coach feedback on the very next rep after **3 of 4** corrections given during the session.
- Tolerates repetition without protest across a full 10-minute block.
- Engages with teammates positively during cooperative play (observed, tallied as present or absent per block).

**Systems — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Names her own position in a simple 4-2 lineup on **5 of 6** rotations when asked.
- Steps the correct direction after a side-out on **5 of 6** rotations without coach help.
- Answers "stay or switch?" correctly on **4 of 6** prompts (overlap rules taught conceptually, not rigorously) [aoc-2021-teach-rotations-beginners].

**Athletic — promotion-ready demonstrates:**
- Multi-directional movement (shuffle, drop step) through a 4-cone pattern **twice** without falling or losing balance.
- Lands jumps with knees-over-toes and bent-knee absorption on **8 of 10** observed landings.

**Promotion gate (one gate, stated identically here and on [[tryout-rubric-11s]]).**

> An 11s athlete promotes to [[12s]] on **3 or higher on at least 7 of the 9 rubric rows, with no score of 1 in Passing or Serving.**

On the rubric's total-score bands this is the **27-35 / on-track** band and above, though the row count is the gate and the bands are only a reading aid. Two clarifications that resolve how the rubric has been misread:

- **Column 3 is the promotion signal, not column 5.** Column 3 is the on-track 11s state. An athlete sitting at 3s across the board is exactly what an 11s graduate is supposed to look like, and she promotes.
- **Column 5 flags a higher-team or accelerated-grouping conversation.** It is the [[12s]] column-3 standard restated. It is a scouting note, not a bar to clear, and nothing in column 5 is 11s install content.

Athletics is a rubric row but not a gate row: an 11-year-old mid-growth-spurt can score 2 on movement quality in October and 4 in March, and that swing says nothing about whether she belongs at 12s.

A player clearing this gate is ready for [[12s]], where the platform must become stable, the standing-float serve must be reliable, the offense graduates from 4-2 install to a simple [[offense-6-2]] by season's end, and overhead passing is added on free balls.

## Season operations

**Checkpoints.** Re-score the same nine rows of [[tryout-rubric-11s]] at **week 6** and **week 12**. Same rows, same anchors, same numbers off this page. Changing the instrument mid-season means the second score cannot be compared to the first.

**Behind standard.** For an athlete under 3 on any row: pick **her lowest two criteria only**, add one named extra-rep block from [[11s-drills]] against them (for the overhand serve that is [[munciana-kneel-single-leg-serve]]; for defense it is [[munciana-speedball]]), and re-check in two weeks. Run that cycle **twice** before raising it with a parent. Most 11s shortfalls close on rep volume alone, and a parent conversation opened in week 4 turns a rep problem into an identity problem.

**Error-correction priority.** One correction per rep, in this order: **feet before platform, toss before swing, approach before arm swing, landing before contact height.** The cause is always earlier in the sequence than the thing that looked wrong. An 11s passer who shanked the ball almost never has a platform problem; she has a feet problem that arrived at the platform.

## Common coaching corrections

- **Reaching with the platform instead of moving the feet.** Cue: "Move your feet first, platform second" — Munciana's pursuit-shuffle drill addresses this directly [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Setter thumbs colliding above forehead.** Cue: "Triangle window — see the ball through the window" [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals].
- **Hitter approaching too fast and out of control.** Cue: "Start slow, accelerate the last two steps" [aoc-2024-attack-approach].
- **Overhand server tossing inconsistently.** Drill the toss alone — 10 tosses, no swing, ball must land at the same spot each time [aoc-2024-kids-serving-fundamentals].
- **Players standing flat-footed on defense.** Cue from the program gloss on Munciana's Three P's: "Get low" before the ball comes [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Reaching back to the net during blocking.** Hands above net, fingers spread, do not retreat — backing up creates the gap.
- **Server cleating the net or court.** Move her forward one step in the published serving-line progression until contact is clean, then earn the distance back. Distance is gated on contact quality, never on the calendar.

## Recommended drills

The 11U list inherits the 10U warm-up + cooperative ball-control core and adds **overhand-serve drills, simple shuttle-passing (2-line), and 2-step approach drills**. Full curated list at [[11s-drills]]; must-do core:

- **Pursuit & footwork:** [[munciana-two-man-shuffle]], [[dynamic-warmup-volleyball]], [[jump-warmup]] (movement quality, not max-vertical).
- **Passing:** [[butterfly-passing]] (shortened distance), [[two-line-passing]], [[shuttle-passing]], [[pepper]]. Lingenfelter's short-ball/long-ball warmup with 3 passers is age-appropriate [aoc-2021-lingenfelter-short-ball-long-ball].
- **Setting:** [[target-setting]] (catch-and-set for most, live for ready candidates). Newkirk's toss-up → fizzles → four-sets progression is well-suited [aoc-2021-youth-15-drills].
- **Hitting:** [[approach-and-swing]] with 2-step Flamingo [aoc-2024-kids-attacking-fundamentals]; down-ball reps from puppet position.
- **Serving:** [[munciana-kneel-single-leg-serve]] for the install, then [[serve-targets]] run from the attack line per the published serving-line progression, [[zone-serving]] as concept. Newkirk's "Deliver and Pop" works for the overhand introduction [aoc-2021-youth-15-drills].
- **Blocking:** [[block-footwork-ladder]] (14U adaptation: shuffle and crossover patterns only, no swing-run) so the blocking row on [[tryout-rubric-11s]] is a skill the team has actually rehearsed.
- **Defense:** [[munciana-speedball]] as the day's first defense rep, catch-and-throw only,, which trains the read-and-run the rubric scores without loading platform mechanics [munciana-2022-camp-drills].
- **Mixed / cooperative:** [[cooperative-25-goal]] (Kessel-style net game) [usav-2026-growing-kids-volleyball], [[pass-set-hit]] in slow form, and Loser-Becomes-the-Net (a cooperative game where the pair that errs becomes the "net" for the next pair) when nets are unavailable [usav-2026-youth-volleyball-tips].
- **Selectivity note:** Munciana's drill library is selectively appropriate at 11U — opening pursuit drills work cleanly, but 6v6 psychology games (Biggie Smalls, Prove It, Tug of War) are 13U+ material — cognitive pressure exceeds 11U capacity.

## Practice ratio across the season

The blocked / small-group / 4v4 mix for 11s runs **40/55/5 → 30/55/15 → 30/50/20 → 25/45/30 → 35/50/15** across preseason, mid-season, late-season, pre-tournament-peak, and taper respectively. 11s shares 10s's small-group dominance (50-55% bucket 2 baseline) but with skill development pulling ahead — the platform and contact mechanics are stable enough to graduate beyond catch-and-set, so blocked work is more efficient per minute [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals]. **What bucket 3 contains at 11s.** In this program, **11s competition is 6v6 all season** (program decision, confirmed 2026-07), so bucket 3 is **6v6 game play in the [[offense-4-2]]** from the first week it can be run: rotations installed early, then 6v6 blocks carry the competition prep. USAV's codified small-sided defaults through age 11 [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules] remain the *practice-design* baseline and live in bucket 2 (2v2/3v3/4v4 small-group formats build contacts-per-minute), and small-court 4v4 game play is a legitimate bucket-3 bridge in the first weeks before rotations are installed. The 4-2 is a six-rotation offense and cannot be evaluated in a 4v4 format, so **the 4-2 promotion criteria are evaluated in the 6v6 blocks** and nowhere else. The 30% pre-tournament-peak ceiling is a ceiling on competition-format minutes overall. Drill-format anchors: [[butterfly-passing]], [[serve-targets]] (blocked); [[serve-receive-3v3]], [[queen-of-the-court-passing]], [[wash-drill]] 3v3, [[cooperative-25-goal]] (small-group); 6v6 4-2 blocks, with 4v4 simulation only as the early-season bridge (bucket 3). See [[practice-ratios]] for the full matrix, bucket definitions, and adjacent-age trajectories.

## Recommended cues

The 11U cue library is intentionally **small and consistent**. Per Moran et al. 2023, cue-type optimization is not robustly supported by youth data — consistent vocabulary repeated until the motor pattern is stable matters more [moran-2023-verbal-coaching-cues-youth].

- **Passing:** "Stopped and balanced" (Kiraly) [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass]; "Thumbs together"; "Move your feet first, platform second"; "Quiet platform" (Munciana) [aoc-2025-cc-skill-training-clubwide]. See [[passing-cues]].
- **Setting:** "Triangle window above the forehead"; "Legs make the set, hands shape it" [aoc-2024-kids-setting-fundamentals]; "Hand-hinge" for ready candidates [aoc-2024-setting-hand-hinge]. See [[setting-cues]].
- **Hitting:** "Start slow, accelerate the last two steps" (Schmidt) [aoc-2024-attack-approach]; "Elbow up, hand high"; "Contact in front." See [[hitting-cues]].
- **Blocking:** "Hands above the net, fingers spread"; "Don't back up." See [[blocking-cues]].
- **Serving:** "Toss to the same spot every time"; "Aim small, miss small" (Munciana) [munciana-2022-camp-drills]; "Reach up, drop, pop" [aoc-2021-youth-15-drills]. See [[serving-cues]].
- **Defense:** "Get back, get low, get a touch," the program gloss on Munciana's Three P's, which in Lingenfelter's own words are Posture, Position, Passion [munciana-2022-camp-drills]; "Eyes up." See [[defense-cues]].
- **Transition:** "Where do you go next?"; "Recover to your spot." See [[transition-cues]].
- **Recency bias:** the cue library leans toward **modern AOC + Munciana** language as primary, with USAV CAP defaults as the developmental scaffold. Legacy cues ("snap your wrist" for hitting; isolated-platform passing language) are avoided at 11U because they install patterns that become anti-cues later.
