# Volleyball serving tips: Target the seams for tougher serves

## Summary

Jim Stone (Ohio State 1982 to 2007, USA Youth National Team 2008 to 2019) argues against the standard "serve zone X" instruction and reframes the server's target as the seam rather than the zone. His reasoning is perceptual and mechanical: a ball delivered into a zone that already contains a passer arrives at that passer, and a passer receiving a ball at her body is not stressed. A ball in a seam forces lateral movement, forces a call between two players, and degrades platform quality.

The piece also lays out the geometry explicitly. In a three-passer serve-receive formation there are four seams available (two interior seams between adjacent passers, two exterior seams between an end passer and her sideline), which gives the server a concrete four-target menu in place of the six-zone map. Stone layers depth on top: short serves in the seam pull a passer into the front-row traffic, deep serves in the seam force a high contact that produces shanks and low-quality passes.

Stone frames the entire idea as a post-fundamentals layer. Servers earn the seam menu after the float serve mechanics are stable, and the drill he recommends for building the accuracy is Ray Bechard's placement serving drill, scored so that points are awarded for serves that land away from the passer.

## Key claims / ideas

- Serving directly into an occupied zone does not stress a serve-receive team, because most passers handle a ball played at their body. This holds for both float serves and jump serves.
- The server's job is redefined as making passers move laterally: "If (the server) can get people moving sideways to pass, they've done their job."
- A three-passer formation exposes four seams: passer-to-sideline on the left, left-to-middle, middle-to-right, and passer-to-sideline on the right. That is the tactical target list, in place of the six numbered zones.
- Depth is an independent axis that compounds with the seam. Short into the seam creates front-row crowding and confusion; deep into the seam forces a high contact and raises shank rate.
- Seam serving is layered after float-serve fundamentals are established, rather than taught as the entry-level serving target.
- Accuracy for the seam target is trainable through scored placement work in which points come from serving away from the target player.
