# BSBH Variations

## Summary

Trinsey untangles the two things coaches mean by BSBH: the ball-setter-ball-hitter eye sequence itself, and the family of drills used to train it. He credits Gold Medal Squared with popularizing the term (Carl McGown specifically), walks the eye sequence handoff by handoff, then gives the operational core of the piece: how he builds BSBH rounds out of a deliberate mix of scripted, semi-scripted, and live reps.

The drill architecture is the most transferable part. Coach-initiated entries exist to control difficulty for beginners, whose real problem is "failure without feedback": the chaos of 6v6 overwhelms their visual processing so completely that they do not know why the rep failed. Trinsey escalates the read demand in stages, starting with the single question "is it coming over?" and only later layering the Off/On/Over distinction and set-direction reads. For teams past beginner level he abandons the setter-tosses-to-coach entry as clunky and enters a ball across the net to a passer instead.

## Key claims / ideas

- Trinsey: BSBH is the eye sequence he trains for blockers and defenders, and the term is long associated with Gold Medal Squared. He does not know whether Carl McGown coined it, and credits him with popularizing it.
- Trinsey: blockers who follow the pass all the way into the setter's hands react slower to the set than blockers already on the setter before the ball arrives. He coaches the handoff as "Ball-SetterSetterSetter" to force it early.
- Trinsey: the "Ball-Hitter" half of the sequence is genuinely contested. Some coaches keep blockers on the ball longer to line up with it, others get on the hitter earlier, and he leaves the question open rather than resolving it.
- Trinsey: with beginner teams he gets most of his mileage from the single first read, "is it coming over," giving deliberately obvious visual cues with his platform before adding any further distinction.
- Trinsey: the read ladder after that is Off / On / Over. For beginners Off means the ball gets bump-set to the outside or whoever is closest, On means a dump or a middle set. For advanced teams the same Off/On read becomes in-system versus out-of-system.
- Trinsey: his standard format is 8-ball rounds. Eight points of 6v6 runs about 4 minutes at roughly 30 seconds per rally, so 4 rounds fit in a little over 15 minutes and 8 rounds in a little under 40.
- Trinsey: a go-to round is balls 1 and 2 scripted or semi-scripted, balls 3 and 4 live serves, balls 5 and 6 scripted or semi-scripted, balls 7 and 8 live serves.
- Trinsey: scripted reps are easier rather than better. They remove a decision so attention can go somewhere else, and the live reps are what the scripted reps feed into, because live is where learning actually happens.
