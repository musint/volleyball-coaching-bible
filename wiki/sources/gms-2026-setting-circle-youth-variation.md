# Setting Circle: Youth Variation

## Summary

A drill card from the GMS community's youth-coaching space describing a
coach-initiated setting circle. Athletes set to a target, then follow their ball
through a fixed role rotation.

The design detail that makes it youth-appropriate is the follow-your-ball
rotation: set, become the target, shag, return to setting. Every athlete cycles
through the receiving role, which keeps the line moving and gives each player a
turn holding the target position, and the shag step is built into the rotation
rather than left to whoever is nearest. That structure is what keeps a young
group from stacking up in a static line.

The coach controls difficulty through two variables, toss location and target
location, which means one drill covers everything from a stationary set to a
moving set to an off-target set without changing the setup.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: a coach initiates the drill with a toss, and the first athlete sets to
  target.
- GMS: athletes follow their ball, constantly moving to the next spot. The role
  rotation is set, then become target, then shag the ball, then back to setting.
- GMS: coaches vary the location of the toss and also practice setting to
  different targets, which are the two difficulty levers built into the drill.
- GMS: it is filed as the youth variation of the setting circle, so the
  coach-initiated toss replaces a player-initiated entry.

## Topics covered

- Youth setting warm-up organization.
- Follow-your-ball role rotation as a line-management device.
- Toss location and target location as difficulty levers.
