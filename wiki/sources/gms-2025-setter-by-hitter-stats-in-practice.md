# Setter-by-Hitter Stats in Practice

## Summary

A follow-up question to Mike Wall's 5-1 versus 6-2 material, asking the
operational question that Wall's rule leaves open: how do you actually collect
setter-by-setter data before the season, in practice, rather than after the fact
in matches?

The post is short and consists mostly of well-formed questions, which is what
makes it useful. It names the three things a coach has to decide before tracking
anything: what sample size is enough before committing to a system, whether to
run the stats per rotation for both a 5-1 and a 6-2 with each setter, and whether
a spreadsheet with rotation rows and OH / MB / OPP columns tallying sets and
kills is sufficient instrumentation.

It documents that coaches are trying to move the system decision onto practice
data rather than waiting for match data. No staff answer is captured.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS (field case): the poster is acting on Mike Wall's position that the coach
  needs to know which setter is most effective at running the offense, and is
  asking how to generate that evidence in practice.
- GMS (field case): three open operational questions are named. How do you track
  these stats in practices, how big should the sample be before making a decision,
  and do you run stats in each rotation of both a 5-1 and a 6-2 per setter in
  order to compare.
- GMS (field case): the proposed instrument is a simple spreadsheet with rows for
  each rotation and columns for OH, MB, and OPP, tallying sets and kills. The
  poster asks whether that is adequate and whether GMS recommends a form.
- GMS (field case): the stated intent is either to identify the most effective
  setter for a 5-1 or to let the data justify a 6-2, which is the decision
  procedure in [[gms-2025-5-1-or-6-2-how-do-you-decide]] applied prospectively.
- No GMS staff answer or recommended form is captured in this snapshot.
