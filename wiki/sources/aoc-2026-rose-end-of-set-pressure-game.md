# End-of-set pressure game

## Summary

Russ Rose (Penn State 1979 to 2021) describes a scoring-manipulation team game whose entire purpose is to buy more reps of the part of a set that decides matches. Ordinary scrimmaging to 25 produces one end-of-set sequence per game. Rose's format produces three, by starting each of three games to 15 at an already-late score.

The mechanics are simple. Evenly matched teams start in base position. Three games to 15 are played, beginning at 9-9, then 11-11, then 13-13. The coach initiates each rally with a free ball or down ball to the side that won the previous point, and the rally is played out.

The initiation rule is the design detail worth noting: the ball goes to the team that just scored, which compounds momentum and forces the trailing team to break a run under end-of-set scoreboard pressure. Requirements are 12 or more players, a coach, and a full court.

## Key claims / ideas

- Rose: the purpose of the game is putting teams in pressure situations to score in the final points of a set.
- Rose: run three games to 15, starting respectively at 9-9, 11-11, and 13-13, so every rally sits in the end-of-set scoring window.
- Rose: teams on both sides should be evenly matched and start in base position.
- Rose: the coach initiates each rally with a free ball or down ball to the side that won the last point, and teams play the ball out to score.
- Rose: the format requires 12 or more players, a coach, and a full court.
- Manipulating the starting score is the mechanism that raises the density of high-leverage reps, since a game played from 0 produces only one late-set sequence.
