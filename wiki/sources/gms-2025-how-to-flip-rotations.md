# How To Flip Rotations During Volleyball Drills

## Summary

A short operational guide taught at GMS clinics and summer camps: a specific
six-round rotation sequence for 6v6 drills that keeps a middle blocker from
sitting front row for two consecutive rotations.

This is a load-management detail disguised as a drill-administration detail, and
it is the kind of thing that quietly shapes a season. The default sequences most
coaches use, either "1v1, 2v2, 3v3" or "switch front row with back row," leave a
middle front row twice in a row, which is exhausting for the front-row middle and
leaves the off middle standing around. The GMS sequence solves both at once and
also produces more varied pin matchups.

The guide is candid about the one thing the sequence does not fix: middles always
end up matched against the same opposing middle, and that has to be solved by
shuffling player assignments between drills rather than by changing the sequence.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: the recommended 6v6 drill rotation sequence, by round, is Team A in
  rotation 3 against Team B in 2, then A6 against B5, A4 against B1, A1 against
  B4, A5 against B6, and A2 against B3.
- GMS: the most important reason for the sequence is to prevent a middle blocker
  from being in the front row for two consecutive rotations, which is exhausting
  for the front-row middle and boring for the off middle, especially in rotations
  where the off middle is neither serving nor playing defense.
- GMS: the payoff is fresher and healthier middles both within the drill and
  across the course of a season.
- GMS: a secondary benefit is more varied individual front-row matchups across
  the team than either a generic "1v1, 2v2, 3v3" sequence or an even more generic
  "switch front row with back row" sequence produces.
- GMS: the known limitation is that middles are always matched against the same
  opposing middle, a direct consequence of never leaving a middle front row twice
  in a row. Solve it by systematically rotating teams and the M1/M2 and OH1/OH2
  assignments from drill to drill or practice to practice, rather than by changing
  the rotation sequence.
