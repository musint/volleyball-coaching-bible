# How to foster a program culture

## Summary

Jared Rudiger (head volleyball coach, Fort Collins High School) names three pillars of program culture: equal treatment, family, and work ethic. The piece is short and prescriptive, and its distinguishing move is where it puts equal treatment.

Equal treatment in Rudiger's framing is not about playing time. It is about respect, expectation, and developmental opportunity, all of which he holds constant across skill level. A bench player and a starter are treated with the same respect, held to the same work expectation, and given equal opportunity to improve their skills. That decouples the culture standard from the depth chart, which is the part a program can actually hold itself to.

Family is framed instrumentally as well as affectively: belonging and accountability travel together, and the reason to build them is that athletes then compete and perform for one another. Work ethic is a coach-side declaration, stated explicitly and applied to both the court and the weight room.

## Key claims / ideas

- Rudiger: equal treatment means every athlete regardless of skill level receives the same respect, is held to the same hard-work expectation, and gets equal opportunity to improve her skills.
- Rudiger: instill a sense of family, belonging, and accountability in the program, and the payoff is that athletes compete and perform for one another rather than for themselves.
- Rudiger: belonging and accountability are paired, so the family frame carries an obligation as well as a welcome.
- Rudiger: the coach must state the work-ethic expectation explicitly, and it applies every time an athlete steps on the court or into the weight room.
- Culture standards are held constant across the depth chart, which separates the culture contract from playing-time decisions.
