# Youth setter training progression

## Summary

A five-step setting progression from Steve Willingham (Lakeville South High School, Minneapolis) built around one constraint: young athletes lack the strength that club and high school setters have, so the progression removes the distance and load requirement first and adds it back last.

The sequence starts seated, which eliminates the legs from the equation entirely and forces the hands to do the work over a short distance. It then adds self-toss while still seated, so the athlete controls her own feed. Only at step three does the athlete stand, and step three is footwork alone with no ball outcome to chase. Steps four and five restore the full task at the 10-foot line, first off a self-toss and then off a bounce, which is the step that introduces a moving ball the setter did not place herself.

The design is legible as a constraints ladder: seated to standing, self-fed to bounce-fed, short distance to across-the-net. Each step changes exactly one variable. ## Key claims / ideas

- Willingham: setting is hard for young athletes primarily because they are not as strong as club and high school players, which is the constraint the progression is designed around.
- Step 1: the athlete sits on the ground, holds the ball, and shoot sets to a partner, isolating hand mechanics with the legs removed.
- Step 2: still seated, the athlete self-tosses and then shoot sets to a partner, adding ball control to the isolated hand work.
- Step 3: the athlete stands on the 10-foot line and works footwork alone, with no set outcome attached.
- Step 4: from the 10-foot line the athlete self-tosses and sets across the net to a partner on the opposite 10-foot line.
- Step 5: from the 10-foot line the athlete bounces the ball and sets across the net off the bounce, which is the first rep where the ball arrives on a trajectory she did not fully determine.
