# 5 keys to effective practice planning

## Summary

Glenn Hoag (Arka Sports Club, Turkey; former Canada men's national team head coach) gives a five-stage planning framework that runs from athlete assessment to drill prescription: status, demands, context, forecasting, prescribing.

The sequence is deliberately ordered. Status asks where the athlete actually is (beginner or experienced, age, sex, background). Demands asks what the sport itself requires and how that differs from training athletes in other sports. Context is the resource audit: practices per week, number of balls, number of courts, all of which constrain what any plan can realistically contain. Forecasting sets goals for athletes and team across time. Prescribing is the last stage and the only one that names drills, rep counts, and weight-room sessions.

Two operational specifics come out of it. Hoag recommends planning in month-long cycles of roughly eight practices, with goals set for the block, so a weakness like serve receive gets addressed across the cycle rather than in a single session. He also places new teaching at the beginning of practice, when athletes are fresh and retention is higher.

## Key claims / ideas

- Hoag: the starting point for planning is "status," determining where the athlete actually is, including experience level, age, and sex.
- Hoag: the second consideration is "demands," what playing volleyball specifically requires and how it differs from training athletes in another sport.
- Hoag: "context" is the resource constraint set, practices per week, balls available, courts available, and it directly shapes what a plan can contain.
- Hoag: "forecasting" means setting goals for athletes and the team over time, ahead of choosing any drill.
- Hoag: "prescribing" comes last and covers drill selection, rep counts, and weight-room volume.
- Hoag: "I recommend you work with month-long cycles," roughly eight practices, with goals set for the block so a team weakness such as serve receive gets sustained attention.
- Hoag: introduce new teaching toward the beginning of practice, when athletes are fresh and more likely to retain the information.
