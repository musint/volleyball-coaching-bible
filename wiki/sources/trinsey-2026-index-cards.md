# Index Cards

## Summary

A short operational piece on how Trinsey carries a practice plan when he has no whiteboard. Having launched his own club and lost access to a facility whiteboard, he moved to writing an index card per coach: Big Rocks in the top-right corner of the front, then the practice schedule with 1 or 2 coaching emphases written in blue next to each drill.

The load-bearing idea is that the drill sequence is not what requires attention. Trinsey runs standing practice templates he knows cold, so the card exists to hold the thing that changes practice to practice: what each coach is actually coaching inside each activity. Writing a second card for the assistant, with different emphases from the head coach's card, is his substitute for the pre-practice staff meeting club programs never have time for.

## Key claims / ideas

- Trinsey: he plans practice blocks in a notebook, then transfers them to a whiteboard or an index card, and runs practice off the card. A notebook is too big and clunky to run practice from and gets lost when set down.
- Trinsey: the real content of the card is the 1 or 2 coaching emphases per drill. The timing and flow of practice he can run in his sleep off a go-to template; what requires mindful engagement is what you are coaching in each activity.
- Trinsey: he writes a separate card for each assistant, with deliberately different coaching tasks per block, so coaches do not step on each other's toes and each has a clear focus.
- Trinsey: many club head coaches under-use their assistants because assistants are less experienced and there is no time for the lengthy pre-practice meetings a college staff would have. A written card closes that gap.
- Trinsey: writing both cards took him 8 minutes 26 seconds, self-timed.
- Trinsey: he spends little time planning practice because he front-loads the season by building out Big Rocks and a consistent template, then brings it to life in a few minutes per session.
- Trinsey: players being able to see the practice plan is nice to have and not critical.
- Trinsey: even as an assistant on college and pro staffs he wrote his own cards to remind himself what the head coach had tasked him with.
