# Serve-Receive Ladder

## Summary

A GMS drill-library entry describing a laddered version of split-court serving
and passing. The court is split in half and played in both directions, which
yields four simultaneous games and a high rep count per athlete.

The base structure rotates three roles (server, passer, target) so every player
serves to and receives from both of the others. The ladder layer adds promotion
and relegation across four courts, which turns a rep drill into a ranked
competition without adding any equipment or coaching overhead.

Its practical use is broader than skill training. The ladder format produces a
ranked ordering of passers as a byproduct, which makes it a natural tryout
instrument as well as a practice drill.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: the drill is a variation on split-court serving and passing. Split the
  court in half and play both directions, which creates four simultaneous games
  and maximizes reps.
- GMS: three positions per group, server, passer, and target. Play for a fixed
  period (for example five minutes) or a fixed number of serves (for example ten),
  then rotate positions so each player serves to the other two and receives serves
  from the other two.
- GMS: to ladder the drill, designate courts 1 through 4. The winner of each group
  moves up a court into the lowest spot in that group, the loser moves down into
  the highest spot in that group, and the middle player stays.
- GMS: ties go to the player who was highest on the ladder at the start of the
  round.
- GMS: several scoring formats work with this setup; the group structure and the
  promotion rule are the fixed parts.
