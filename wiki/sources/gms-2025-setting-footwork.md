# Setting Footwork

## Summary

A GMS discussion-board post reporting on a Jim Stone newsletter video series that
presents setting footwork two ways: a left-foot-lead series of six patterns and a
right-foot-lead series of four. Stone's own recommendation, as relayed here, is
to teach all of them and let players decide which to use.

The post is short and ends on an open question rather than an answer, so its
value is in documenting the disagreement rather than resolving it. The posting
coach teaches only the right-foot-lead pattern and finds it more rhythmic and
simple, which puts a concrete practitioner position next to Stone's
teach-everything position.

Because the reply thread is not captured, this snapshot records the question and
the two stated positions without the community response.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS (relaying Jim Stone): Stone's video series presents both a left-foot-lead
  setting footwork series with six patterns and a right-foot-lead series with four
  patterns.
- GMS (relaying Jim Stone): Stone's recommendation is to teach all of the
  patterns and let the players decide which one to use.
- GMS: the posting coach teaches only the right-foot-lead pattern, learned from
  "Jim Mac" and Courtney, and finds it more rhythmic and simple.
- GMS: the lead-foot question is open in the community. The post asks how many
  coaches teach left-foot-lead versus right-foot-lead patterns and no consensus
  answer is captured in this snapshot.

## Topics covered

- Setting footwork lead-foot convention.
- Left-foot-lead versus right-foot-lead pattern inventories.
- Teach-one versus teach-all-and-let-them-choose as a methodological split.
