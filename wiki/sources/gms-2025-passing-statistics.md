# Everything You Should Know About Passing Statistics

## Summary

GMS's position paper on how to grade passing. It documents both common average-
grade scales, names their practical failure modes, and then argues for a single
simpler stat: good pass percentage, the share of a passer's attempts that are
good or perfect.

The argument is empirical rather than aesthetic. GMS compares passing-average
rank against good-pass-percentage rank for three programs at three levels, the
USA Women's National Team and the University of Washington via VolleyMetrics and
Mountain View High School via HUDL, and reports that the rank orders are almost
exactly the same, with variance appearing only where the underlying differences
are minuscule. If the simple stat ranks passers the same way the complex one
does, GMS's position is that the complexity buys nothing.

This is the source to cite for GMS's stance on pass grading, and it is directly
relevant to the wiki's 0-3 scale, which GMS here calls the more commonly used
of the two average-grade scales.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: "For passing stats, keep it simple." That is both the opening and the
  closing line of the article.
- GMS: documents the 4-point average passing grade (perfect 4, good 3, medium 2,
  poor 1, ace 0) and the 3-point average passing grade (perfect 3, good 2, poor 1,
  ace 0), and calls the 3-point version "perhaps more commonly used."
- GMS: average-grade stats have two practical problems. They are often difficult
  to keep consistently in real time, because the statistician has to determine and
  remember a grade for each pass while the rally continues, and the resulting
  decimals confuse young players, who understand that higher is better but cannot
  interpret the difference between a 2.05 and a 1.95 average.
- GMS: college coaches use an even wider variety of passing stats, each more
  detailed than the last, which compounds both problems.
- GMS: the recommended stat is good pass percentage, the total percentage of a
  player's passes that are good, with a perfect pass counted as good. Ten
  attempts with six good or perfect is 60 percent.
- GMS (Chris McGown): "This really simple stat captures all the complexities of
  passing."
- GMS: good pass percentage rank correlates almost exactly with passing average
  rank across USA Women's National Team data (VolleyMetrics), University of
  Washington data (VolleyMetrics), and Mountain View High School data (HUDL). The
  only rank variance occurs where the raw difference is extremely small.
- GMS: the recommendation covers both collection and communication. Use the
  simple stat when taking passing stats in practices and matches and when sharing
  them with players, because it makes the coach's job easier and the player's
  interpretation clearer.
