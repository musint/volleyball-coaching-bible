# Preseason Week 1.1: Passing

## Summary

The first passing session in GMS's published preseason practice-plan series. It
is a skeleton rather than a script: a warm-up sequence, five passing keys, three
practice activities, and four objectives.

Its value to this wiki is as a record of what GMS actually opens a season with,
and specifically of the five passing keys taught in week one. Those keys are
terse enough to function as a cue set, and they are ordered from contact
mechanics ("wrists and hands together") through platform work ("face ball, angle
platform") to movement and reading ("shuffle," "tracking: see server, see spin").
The reading key appearing in week one is notable given how often reading is
treated as an advanced layer.

The activity list is equally informative about GMS's practice architecture:
warm-up work is cooperative and partner-based, then Butterfly for keyed
repetition, Doghouse for ball-control volume, and Opposite Volleyball to finish
game-like.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: the week-one passing warm-up sequence is one-way partner passing (wrists
  and hands together), a linear pass plus set, triangle pepper, and dig to self /
  set / attack.
- GMS: the five week-one passing keys are wrists and hands together; straight and
  simple; face ball, angle platform; shuffle; and tracking, stated as "see server,
  see spin."
- GMS: the practice activities are Butterfly The Passers (three variations),
  Doghouse (two variations), and Opposite Volleyball.
- GMS: the session objectives are to define the demands and mechanics of passing,
  to review or introduce the passing keys during warm-up, to use Butterfly to
  focus and fine-tune the keys, and to use Doghouse for ball-control reps.
- GMS: reading the server is a week-one key rather than a later addition, and it
  is framed at the level of ball information ("see server, see spin") rather than
  as tactical anticipation.
