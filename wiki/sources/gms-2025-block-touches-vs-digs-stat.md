# Why aren't block touches counted like digs in volleyball statistics?

## Summary

A discussion-board post making a measurement argument that is worth recording
even though nobody answers it in this snapshot: the block statistic as
conventionally kept counts only blocks that produce a point, and under that
definition blocking is the skill least correlated with winning.

The poster's proposition is that the definition is doing the work. A block touch
that does not end the rally but keeps the ball playable is, in his observation,
highly correlated with winning, and it is simply not tracked. He flags his own
evidence as anecdotal and a study as pending.

This is a direct measurement-side companion to
[[gms-2025-blocking-importance]], which reports the same weak correlation and
asks a lineup question about it. Together they frame a real open question in the
wiki's stats material: whether blocking's low correlation with winning is a fact
about blocking or a fact about how blocking is counted.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS (field case): the block statistic as conventionally kept counts a block
  only when it results in a point.
- GMS (field case): under that definition, blocking is the skill least correlated
  with winning, which the poster treats as established.
- GMS (field case): the poster's anecdotal position is that a block touch which
  does not result in a point but allows play to continue is highly correlated with
  winning. He explicitly flags this as his own observation with a study pending.
- GMS (field case): the open question is historical and methodological. Why are
  blocks not stat-kept the way digs are, where the touch itself is the recorded
  event rather than the outcome.
- No GMS staff answer is captured in this snapshot.

## Topics covered

- Definition of the block statistic and what it excludes.
- Block touches versus block points as recorded events.
- Whether measurement choice explains blocking's weak correlation with winning.
- Asymmetry between how digs and blocks are counted.
