# 13 tryout tips for high school coaches

## Summary

Tod Mattox (The Bishop's School, La Jolla) gives the most operationally complete tryout protocol in the AOC catalog: eligibility rules, an intake note card, athletic testing, four evaluation lenses, three scored drills written out in full, cut structure, staff process, and the mechanics of delivering the news. At roughly 2,000 words it is a procedure rather than a tip list.

Two ideas do the most work. The first is his tiered meritocracy: for the top 7 to 9 players selection is purely best-player-plays regardless of grade, and below that line a younger athlete who would sit on varsity is deliberately placed on JV to be a go-to player instead. Mattox defends this on leadership-development grounds rather than on skill grounds, and he acknowledges the athlete and parent will usually want the opposite. The second is learning rate as an evaluation criterion: when two candidates look identical in August they will look different in November, so he tests who will hold an uncomfortable technical correction (elbows extended, hands flat on the set finish) versus who defers to what feels comfortable, and the faster learner gets the varsity spot.

His drills are built for evaluation rather than for training. All three use ladder or doghouse formats with low player-to-ball ratios, so every candidate accumulates touches and produces a comparable score. He also warns that 6 on 6 evaluation is distorted by dominant players and pulls the best athletes off the court so bubble players get real looks.

## Key claims / ideas

- Mattox: for the top 7 to 9 roster spots selection is a true meritocracy regardless of grade level; for spots 10 to 14 a sophomore who would not start on varsity is placed on JV as a go-to player, which is defended as better leadership development than varsity bench time.
- Mattox: learning rate is a primary evaluation criterion, because two players similar in August will be different by November; test it by asking for an uncomfortable technical finish and observing who holds it.
- Mattox: collect a note card from every athlete before or on day one with experience, prior club, best-coach-and-why, self-identified best position, and willingness to play other positions, specifically so a candidate is not stranded at a position she has never played.
- Mattox: run at least two cuts, so evaluators can focus on the real bubble and so stronger candidates read the process as gradual rather than being lumped in with players who were never close.
- Mattox: vertical jump (approach touch minus standing reach) and a shuttle run are the two athletic tests worth the tryout time.
- Mattox: look for upper-body torque on the swing, with the chest turned sideways and hips rotating through, and he attributes the shoulder-and-back health rationale to John Dunning.
- Mattox: evaluating 6 on 6 is distorted because better players dominate, so remove the best players to give bubble candidates opportunities.
- Mattox: involve assistants in final decisions but withhold your own opinion first, so their reads are not anchored to yours.
- Mattox: deliver cuts in 30-second one-on-one meetings during a 20 to 30 minute block while the rest of the group plays a fun game, rather than posting a list, to preserve player dignity.
