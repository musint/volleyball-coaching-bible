# Serving: 2x2x2x2

## Summary

A GMS drill-library entry for a serving-accuracy drill built on four
origin-to-destination combinations. The name is the protocol: two serves each of
four zone pairings.

What distinguishes it from generic serve-target work is that it varies the
serving position as well as the target. Most target drills hold the server in one
spot and move the target; this one crosses both, so the athlete has to solve
Zone 1 to Zone 5 and Zone 5 to Zone 1 as well as the two straight-ahead versions.
The stated benefit is that server effectiveness rises as the ability to serve
from and to different zones increases.

It is short, self-scoring, and needs nothing but balls, which makes it a
reasonable fixed block in a serving-focused practice at almost any age.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: the goal is for athletes to become comfortable serving from multiple spots
  on the service line to multiple spots on the opposing side of the court.
- GMS: the protocol is two serves each of Zone 1 to Zone 1, Zone 5 to Zone 5,
  Zone 1 to Zone 5, and Zone 5 to Zone 1.
- GMS: run it for roughly 5 to 10 minutes. It can be played to a set score or for
  a set time.
- GMS: the variation is three serves of each type instead of two.
- GMS: the rationale is that effectiveness for a server increases as her ability
  to serve from different zones and to different zones increases. The emphasis is
  serving to specific locations.
