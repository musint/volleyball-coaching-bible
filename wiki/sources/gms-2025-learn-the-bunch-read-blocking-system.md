# Learn The Bunch Read Blocking System

## Summary

The GMS community republication of the organization's flagship team-blocking
guide, dated 2025-01-30. It is substantially the same article as the undated
[[gms-nd-blocking]] capture already in the wiki, with two additions that the
older capture does not carry: the Keegan Cook / University of Washington
women's spacing and eyework video references, and an explicit tactical section
naming Load and Dedicate as the two tactics GMS recommends at the high school
and club level. Because this version is dated, it is the better citation
anchor for anything that needs a year.

The article covers both the team system and the individual mechanics. GMS
frames bunch read as its preferred blocking setup, taught for over 25 years,
adopted by teams of all levels and genders including top international
programs. The premise is that blockers start bunched toward the middle of the
court and make fast dynamic moves to the pin, which puts two and often three
blockers in front of most sets.

The writer identifies himself in passing as Carl McGown's son ("my dad would
always yell at me about not supervising pre-practice reps"), which is
consistent with Chris McGown, though the post carries no byline. Treated here
as a GMS organizational article rather than attributed to a named author.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS: bunch read is the organization's preferred team blocking system and has
  been taught for over 25 years; teams of all levels and genders, including top
  international programs, have adopted it.
- GMS: five skill areas make a bunch read work: spacing, footwork, armwork,
  eyework, and tactical positioning. Eyework is the most important of the five,
  and eyework development is constrained by how well the blocker can move, so
  footwork is trained first as the enabling layer.
- GMS: baseline spacing is deliberately asymmetric. "Elbows to elbows" on the
  left, "hands to hands" on the right, because the right-side blocker usually
  has to deal with the opponent's best attacker, who gets set a lot.
- GMS: the footwork lexicon is jump in place, 2-step shuffle, 3-step shuffle
  (Q3), 2-step crossover (X2), 3-step crossover (X3). Ranked for getting over
  the net quickly the order is Q3, X2, X3; for distance covered and for height
  it reverses to X3, X2, Q3. X2 is the middle-of-the-road option.
- GMS: hands-down starting position (relaxed at the waist) produces much better
  block moves than hands-up (at face level). Start hands up only against
  opponents who pass well and run their middle fast and often.
- GMS: get straight across the net wherever you are rather than reaching into
  seams. A blocker may upgrade to a better spot if she is long and athletic
  enough to still be across, and she does not get to reach if it takes her
  parallel to the net. Arms stay together; split blocking creates too many bad
  surfaces for the hitter to tool.
- GMS: eyework sequence is off the pass early, on the setter for a long time,
  and on the hitter by the time the blocker jumps. Against fast offenses the
  hitter pickup happens sooner than that.
- GMS: teach with blocked training first. Tell the blockers where the set is
  going and even which move to use ("we are setting left, and Sally, you have to
  make an X2 move"), then add variables and speed. Blockers stressed about the
  read revert to old mechanics.
- GMS: at the high school and club level you need very few tactics. Load (wings
  drop hands and prepare to run to the pin instead of helping in the middle) and
  Dedicate (a shuffle step toward an important attacker taken while the pass is
  in the air, then back onto the setter for the read) are the two recommended.
  Trap and commit are occasional.
- GMS: dose is 15 to 30 minutes a day. Supervise pre-practice blocking reps
  almost every time, because unsupervised reps drift into bad habits. During the
  learning phase, allow athletes to be late to the pin if the read was good.

## Topics covered

- Bunch read as a team blocking system: premise, spacing, and rationale.
- Blocking footwork lexicon and the tradeoffs between Q3, X2, and X3.
- Armwork: starting hand height, arms together, getting across rather than
  reaching.
- Blocker eyework sequence from pass to setter to hitter.
- Blocked-to-variable training progression for block reads.
- Load and Dedicate as the club and high school tactical set.
- Practice dosing and supervision of pre-practice blocking reps.
