# Mini-Study: Win Probability Charts

## Summary

Trinsey models set-level win probability from every score state across three sideout environments (50%, 60%, and 67%), split by whether the team is serving or receiving, and publishes the underlying Excel files. The 50% environment is the one he says fits most club and high school volleyball; 60% fits NCAA and professional women; 67% is realistically only professional men.

The applied section is where the piece earns its place in match prep. Trinsey declines to prescribe tactics directly and instead converts the tables into two decision aids: score states where a set is already lost enough that substituting for playing time costs you little, and "costly run" thresholds where a run swings win probability by 30 or more percentage points. He notes those run thresholds line up anecdotally with when coaches already call timeouts, while flagging that whether timeouts do anything is itself questionable.

## Key claims / ideas

- Trinsey: a 50% sideout environment (both teams win half of rallies serving and half receiving) is the best approximation for lower-level volleyball through most high school and club ball. Strong varsity and strong 16s-and-up club sit slightly higher; NCAA and professional women fit the 60% charts.
- Trinsey: in a 60% sideout environment the 90% win-probability threshold arrives as early as 12-6. The same score in a 50% environment is only 85.9%, so comebacks are meaningfully more available at club level.
- Trinsey: in a 50% environment, leading 24-21 is only a 93.8% win. That comeback lands roughly 1 in 16 times, which means a club coach should expect to see something like it about once a weekend.
- Trinsey: in a 60% environment the 99% thresholds sit at 20-12, 21-13, 22-15, 23-17, and 24-19.
- Trinsey: win probability drops below 15% when down 7 at any score, down 5 with the opponent at 15 or more, or down 4 with the opponent at 20 or more. He frames these as the windows where substituting for playing time does not meaningfully cost you the set.
- Trinsey: the mirrored thresholds for subbing while winning, shifted 1 to 2 points to account for the cost of the substitution itself, are up 8 or 9 at any time, up 6 to 7 when you have 15 or more, and up 5 to 6 when you have 20 or more.
- Trinsey: the runs that cost 30 or more percentage points of win probability are a 6-point run at 0-0, a 5-point run at 5-5, a 4-point run at 12-12, a 3-point run at 17-17, and a 2-point run at 21-21. Allowing one point inside the run stretches those to 7-1 at 0-0, 6-1 at 4-4, 5-1 at 11-11, 4-1 at 16-16, and 3-1 at 20-20.
- Trinsey: in juniors best-of-three, choosing serve or receive to start the match is mathematically irrelevant, since both teams are guaranteed one 25-point set starting each way. Best-of-five gives a slight edge to receiving first, and a small but meaningful edge in a deciding set.
- Trinsey: the model assumes no momentum and that in-set performance carries no information about relative team strength, so it applies best to close margins and overstates comeback odds at extreme ones.
