# Back Setting in Volleyball

## Summary

A short GMS guide by Tom Black, head volleyball coach at the University of
Georgia, giving three keys for the back set. Black opens by saying his urge is
to keep setting as simple as possible, and the piece stays at three keys.

The third key is the one worth the citation. Black says that following the back
set with the eyes tilts the head and creates space between the release point and
the head, which lets the set travel flat and fast to the right-side hitter. He
then draws an explicit sex difference: this matters less for some men's setters
who take the ball at a very high point, and it applies to essentially every
women's setter he has seen. That makes this a women's-game technique source
rather than a general one.

Black closes with an unusual amount of epistemic humility for a technique piece,
noting that setting seems to be the one skill taught 5,000 different ways and
inviting better methods.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS (Tom Black): keep setting as simple as possible; the whole back set reduces
  to three keys.
- GMS (Tom Black): contact on your midline. Take the ball in the same spot as a
  front set, on the midline of the body above the forehead. Deception is a
  benefit, and the more important reason is controlling location.
- GMS (Tom Black): extend and finish the back set just as you finish a front set.
  The finish sits at roughly a 45-degree angle because of the head's relationship
  to the right-side hitter.
- GMS (Tom Black): follow the set with your eyes, which gets the head out of the
  way. The head tilt creates space between release point and head and lets the
  ball travel flat and fast to the right side.
- GMS (Tom Black): this third key is less critical for some men's setters who
  take the ball at a very high point, and it applies to every women's setter he
  has seen.
- GMS (Tom Black): setting is the one skill that seems to be taught 5,000
  different ways, which he offers as a reason to hold the method loosely.

## Topics covered

- Back set contact point and its relationship to the front set.
- Extension and finish angle on the back set.
- Eye-follow as a mechanism for creating release-point space.
- Sex differences in setter contact height and their teaching consequences.
