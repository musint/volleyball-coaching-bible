# Blocking Importance

## Summary

A short discussion-board question that carries one citable GMS artifact: the
poster references the GMS manual as ranking first-ball sideout, transition
offense, and passing as the skills most correlated with winning, and reports a
clinic claim that blocking is one of the least decisive.

The question that follows is a lineup question rather than a statistics question.
If blocking correlates weakly with winning, how do you explain to a team the
choice between a strong right-side blocker who is weak offensively and a
high-percentage attacker who blocks less well?

It is the applied version of the same ranking that
[[gms-2025-swing-blocking-everything-you-need-to-know]] states from the other
direction when it says blocking is not the most important skill in high school
volleyball. Read together, the two show GMS holding a consistent position: teach
blocking well, and know where it sits in the priority order. No staff answer is
captured here.

## Key claims / ideas

- GMS (field case): a clinic attended by the poster stressed that blocking is one
  of the least decisive skills in determining whether a team wins or loses.
- GMS (field case): the GMS manual is cited as showing first-ball sideout,
  transition offense, and passing as the highest-correlated skills.
- GMS (field case): the open coaching question is how to explain the resulting
  lineup tradeoff to a team, between a player who is a strong right-side blocker
  and weak offensively and a player with a high hitting percentage who blocks less
  well.
- No GMS staff answer is captured in this snapshot.

## Topics covered

- Relative correlation of skills with winning.
- FBSO, transition offense, and passing as priority skills.
- Blocking's position in the skill priority order.
- Explaining lineup tradeoffs to a team.
