# 2022 Munciana Camp Drills (Coach Library): Mike Lingenfelter

## Summary

A 64-minute video tutorial in which [[mike-lingenfelter]] walks Munciana camp staff through every drill they will run during a summer Munciana youth camp. The intended audience is camp coaches running Munciana programming, the explicit framing in the opening minutes is "I'm going to go over every drill that will possibly be in your camps to give you a library so you don't have to feverishly scribble down drills and can actually watch your team participate in the camp and make assessments." Drills are demonstrated in the order they would be coached: ball-control / passing → defense → serving → out-of-system setting and hitting → cooperative ball-control → competitive 6v6. The tape carries no blocking instruction. Blocking surfaces only incidentally inside the 6v6 games (one blocker up in Left vs. Left, a scoring callout on a stuffed ball), so this source cannot support blocking-technique claims anywhere on the wiki.

This is a primary source: the speaker is the program's co-director instructing his own staff on his own drill library, captured in his own words. Trust-tier 1.

## Key claims / ideas

- **Pursuit-and-recovery as the foundation.** The library opens with a two-man shuffle drill: hold low, shuffle down and back, no ball, pursuit footwork as the first thing camp athletes do.
- **Closure on the ball.** Lingenfelter repeatedly emphasizes "simple closure on the ball" before adding passing volume, straight-line passing is built around centering the body and finishing on balance.
- **Triangles as the multi-skill format.** Triangle passing, triangle passing off a box B, and the later "tight triangles" setting/hitting drills all use the same three-corner geometry to combine multiple skills in a small space, a Munciana signature.
- **Defense begins with speedball.** Speedball is named explicitly as "the intro to defense" in the camp progression, defense is taught from a games-based ball-up-everywhere starting point, not from isolated platform work.
- **The Three P's of defense: posture, position, passion.** Lingenfelter's words inside Seat-to-Seam at 00:19:45: "Seat to seam is teaching posture, position, and then passion. Now, the three P's of defense, they're going to get back. Have a quick seat. They got to tap their tush on the box." The program's teaching gloss for the same three priorities, standardized as "get back, get low, get a touch," is a Munciana-program rendering rather than Lingenfelter's phrasing on this tape.
- **"Outside foot high" is a perimeter-defense rule, not a blocking rule.** The cue appears inside Three-Way Release (00:14:03 to 00:15:06), run with the DSs and outside hitters as back-court defenders: "Make sure everybody's outside foot is high. ... So that means the foot on the line needs to be ahead of the foot in the court so their bellies are angled back and in the court. This doesn't happen by accident, it has to be taught." "The foot on the line" is the sideline foot; the stagger angles the defender's belly back into the court so she can seal the perimeter and play up the line. The companion cue at 00:15:06 is "Make sure you cut the outside foot."
- **Figure-eight defense and "teacher" drills.** A coaching-driven movement progression with the coach feeding from a fixed location, athletes flowing through a figure-eight pattern that forces the full pursuit footwork inventory.
- **Aim small, miss small for serving.** Munciana's serving philosophy is target-driven from day one: "all too often in serving we just serve to serve, and it becomes somewhat of a dumpster", instead, every serve is at a finite target (down to a 2-inch area) so athletes hold themselves accountable to placement.
- **No-gut serving + five-before-ten.** Rotating serving drills daily under aggressive-mentality framing, repeated emphasis that camp practice must rehearse the *aggressive* serving mindset alongside the mechanic.
- **Tight triangles for setting/hitting integration.** The post-serving section opens with tight triangles as the first setting/hitting drill, small-court, fast-feedback geometry consistent with the rest of the library.
- **Camp progression mirrors a full practice arc.** Pass → defend → serve → set → hit → block → 6v6 mirrors the practice flow Munciana would run with its own teams; the camp library is the club's drill library, not a separate camp-only inventory.

(Specific named drills and their exact mechanics are documented on individual `wiki/drills/munciana-*.md` pages, see Where it's cited.)

## Topics covered

Passing (pursuit, closure, tilt, multi-ball, triangles), defense (speedball intro, Three-Way Release perimeter work, physio reaction, Mountain D and Campfire D, three P's, figure-eight, Go-Stay), serving (kneel and single-leg toss warm-up, noodle and box target accountability, no-gut, five-before-ten, aggressive-mentality, serve-brackets-water practice rule), out-of-system setting and hitting (five-and-five, Mia, two-man and team out-of-system), cooperative ball-control (tight triangles, Sam Dixette, Samurai transition, pursuit-attack, USA 33, Brazilian warm-up, turn-and-burn, pepper variants), competitive 6v6 (Biggie Smalls, Prove It, Momentum, Left vs. Left, Net Six, Hand-to-Hand Combat, 3-2, Tug of War, Finish Strong).

Two coverage gaps worth naming, because both have been over-claimed from this source in the past: the tape contains **no blocking instruction**, and it contains almost **no hitting-technique instruction**. Hitting appears only as live swings inside the out-of-system and cooperative drills, coached for ball control and shot direction ("go down the line as much as possible," "hit a controllable ball") rather than for approach footwork, arm mechanics, or shot selection.
