# Blocking

## Overview

Blocking is the first line of defense and the only skill in volleyball that
directly scores on the attacker, a stuffed ball is a point, a touched ball
is a slowed ball your diggers can handle, a missed block is free real
estate. The modern block asks four things of the athlete simultaneously:
read what the opposing offense is doing, travel efficiently from ready
position to the point of attack, plant and jump in balance, and finish with
hands pressed over the net into the opposing court. Any one of those
breaking down turns the block into a hole in the defense.

The last twenty years have seen the most significant generational shift in
this skill since the introduction of the reach-over rule. Swing blocking, 
arms swinging like a sprinter's through the lateral close and loading for
the jump at the plant, is now the default teaching at most competitive
HS and college programs, replacing the shuffle-and-lift pattern that
dominated for decades. Gold Medal Squared, Art of Coaching Volleyball, and
most national-team programs have moved in this direction together, though
not without dissent (Terry Liskevych calls swing blocking the "flavor of
the month" and argues traditional technique is more durable, and Ken
Murczek's AOC piece questions whether the higher jump actually translates
into the net penetration that matters). Where that debate lands in your
gym is an age question before it is a philosophy question, and the ladder
in `## Teaching by age band` below carries the program's working install
order. That order runs earlier than the shuffle-only-until-HS default
older material on this wiki assumed.

Four schools frame the methodology landscape, and they disagree most
about how reading is trained rather than about the mechanics at the
plant. All four argue around the same anchor sequence, B-S-B-H (ball to
setter to ball to hitter), whose provenance is itself contested and is
handled in the 13-14 cell below. `## Schools of thought` lays out where
AOC, GMS, ecological dynamics, and Japanese training diverge.

## Major subtopics

- **[[blocking-footwork]]**: how the blocker travels from ready position
  to the point of attack. Static shuffle and crossover patterns as the
  literacy layer, swing-block footwork as the modern HS/college default.
  GMS documents a named lexicon (Q2, Q3, X2, X3); AOC teaches swing as
  the modern default at HS and above. The generational shift lives here.
- **[[blocking-hand-position]]**: near-consensus on the target (press
  over the net, fingers spread and stiff, thumbs up, palms angled down),
  live disagreement on starting height, width, and whether the outside
  blocker seals the seam or turns the line in. Stuff, soft block, and
  getting tooled are separated by inches here.
- **[[blocking-read]]**: the perceptual skill that drives everything
  else. B-S-B-H eye-progression, setter keys, hitter keys,
  commit-versus-read for middles, patterned tendencies ("teams set left
  off bad passes"). Every school agrees this is the primary skill and
  disagrees on how to train it.
- **[[blocking-swing]]**: sprint-arms through the lateral close, arms
  loaded at the plant, drive up and over. Mechanically its own pattern
  rather than a faster static block, and installing it is a coaching
  investment that trades against other priorities.

Related drills include [[block-footwork-ladder]], [[block-touch-drill]],
[[commit-block-trigger]], [[read-blocking-progression]], and
[[swing-block-shuffle]]; system-level blocking schemes (bunch-read
vs. dedicated) live at [[read-blocking]], [[commit-blocking]], and
[[swing-blocking]] in the systems catalog.

## Teaching by age band

Each cell says what to install and what to check. Year-precise detail
stays in the age-guides, drill picks, and tryout rubrics linked from the
cell.

### 10-12: Foundation

- **Introduce.** Net geometry, hands up, a controlled two-foot jump.
  Blocking is not a milestone skill at [[10s]]: USAV simplified youth
  rules allow single-player blocking only, and many youth formats
  discourage it [usav-2026-simplified-youth-rules].
- **Develop.** The static shuffle close and the press. Year gates: at
  [[11s]], hands reach at or above the tape at the top of a controlled
  stationary jump (standing reach is irrelevant at this age); at
  [[12s]], "step-close" and "press over" become working vocabulary.
  Mechanics at [[blocking-footwork]] and [[blocking-hand-position]],
  phrasing at [[blocking-cues]], dry footwork volume at
  [[block-footwork-ladder]].
- **Refine.** Late in the [[12s]] season, top-club rosters only:
  swing-block footwork with no jump from the 10-foot line
  [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners]. GMS reaches early
  introduction from a different direction, teaching 3-step crossover
  fundamentals early and raising the allocation with age and strength,
  while keeping the dose small on the argument that blocking's value
  tracks the power of the opposition and every minute on it is a minute
  off serving and receiving
  [gms-2025-swing-blocking-everything-you-need-to-know].
- **Observable promotion criterion ([[12s]]).** Off a coach-entered
  ball, the feet resolve one way or the other, holding at the net or
  dropping off, before the ball crosses the plane, on 8 of 10 entries.
  This is the beginner rung of Trinsey's ladder, where "is it coming
  over?" carries the load before any Off / On / Over distinction
  [trinsey-2026-bsbh-variations]. Drills [[12s-drills]], scored
  [[tryout-rubric-12s]].

### 13-14: Rebuild

Peak height velocity window. Mechanics get re-taught on a body that
changed since last season, and the read is what carries a blocker whose
timing has not caught up.

- **Introduce.** The B-S-B-H eye sequence as working vocabulary at
  [[13s]], and the read as a named skill.
- **Contested: who owns B-S-B-H.** Program default pending on
  attribution language. Kiraly teaches ball to setter to ball to hitter
  in AOC material, and [[blocking-cues]] carries it as the canonical read
  cue [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill][kiraly-1997-championship-volleyball].
  Trinsey reports the term is long associated with Gold Medal Squared and
  credits Carl McGown with popularizing it, without claiming to know who
  coined it [trinsey-2026-bsbh-variations]. Teach the sequence, attribute
  it to the lineage.
- **Develop.** Static-and-shuffle stays primary at [[13s]], with Rosen's
  10-foot-line swing footwork run without the jump
  [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners]. That staged install is
  single-sourced to Rosen at AOC; GMS supports early footwork
  introduction without staging it the same way
  [gms-2025-swing-blocking-everything-you-need-to-know]. Full swing-block
  execution is not a [[13s]] skill.
- **Refine.** At [[14s]], top-club middles finish the progression
  (footwork plus jump) by mid-season and take it into match play, running
  [[swing-block-shuffle]] once the jump is in. Pin blockers stay
  footwork-only all year, jump-add held to late [[15s]]
  [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners][aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking].
- **Contested: when bunch-read enters.** Program default pending. GMS
  teaches bunch read as its default team system for all levels and
  genders, taught for over 25 years and adopted by top international
  programs [gms-2025-learn-the-bunch-read-blocking-system]. Slabe frames
  the same scheme as demanding dynamic, fast pin blockers and best suited
  to advanced levels like college [aoc-2026-bunch-read-blocking]. Trinsey
  argues from below that beginners get most of their mileage from the
  single "is it coming over?" read [trinsey-2026-bsbh-variations]. The
  program introduces the concept at [[14s]] and applies it at [[15s]],
  between GMS and Slabe, with no ruling behind it.
- **Landing safety, non-negotiable from [[14s]].** Run-back block
  landings produce significantly higher knee-valgus moments than
  step-back landings in matched-elite female blockers
  [zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus]. Stick or step-back is the
  default; run-back is a recovery pattern, never a coached habit.
- **Observable promotion criteria ([[14s]]).** Over one 8-ball round,
  the eyes are visibly off the ball and onto the setter before the ball
  reaches the setter's hands: Trinsey coaches the handoff as
  "Ball-SetterSetterSetter" because blockers who follow the pass into the
  hands react slower to the set [trinsey-2026-bsbh-variations]. Second,
  10 of 10 block landings finish as a stick or a step-back. Do not gate
  on reciting the sequence, because an athlete can explain a concept back
  correctly and still not have changed what she is trying to do
  [trinsey-2026-good-intentions]. Drills [[13s-drills]] and
  [[14s-drills]], scored [[tryout-rubric-13s]] and [[tryout-rubric-14u]].

### 15-16: Refinement

- **Develop.** At [[15s]], top-club middles run the full swing block as
  the default at-pin scheme by mid-season and pins add the jump late in
  the year [aoc-2026-swing-blocking-footwork-beginners][aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking].
  Bunch-read moves from concept to applied, chosen per scout against
  opponents whose middle threat is real [aoc-2026-bunch-read-blocking].
- **Refine.** At [[16s]], bunch-read is the team default and the swing
  handles a wide tempo set without giving up tooling room
  [aoc-2026-rosen-swing-blocking-footwork]. Slabe's press supplies the
  finish, penetrating rather than covering [aoc-2024-slabe-blocking].
  Right-side blocking gets a position-specific install
  [aoc-2024-kuhn-block-as-right-side]. See [[read-blocking]] and
  [[swing-blocking]].
- **Keep the tactical menu short.** GMS recommends two tactics at the
  club and high school level, Load and Dedicate
  [gms-2025-learn-the-bunch-read-blocking-system]. Chris McGown's
  four-signal set (load, dedicate, commit, trap) is the ceiling, and he
  reports trap and commit surfaced only a handful of times a season even
  in his men's teams [gms-2025-indoor-blocking-signals]. See
  [[commit-blocking]] and [[commit-block-trigger]].
- **Contested: the Ball-Hitter half of the read.** Program default
  pending. Trinsey calls this half genuinely unresolved among coaches,
  with some keeping blockers on the ball longer to line up with it and
  others getting them onto the hitter earlier, and he declines to settle
  it [trinsey-2026-bsbh-variations]. Coach the first handoff as settled
  and treat the second as per-athlete tuning.
- **Injury load rises.** HS-female ACL prevalence is 9.3%, the highest
  age-band incidence in the volleyball literature
  [sassi-2025-acl-volleyball-systematic-review]. Unanticipated lateral
  block jumps also produce higher vertical ground reaction force and
  reduced knee-flexion power than pre-cued ones, so read-blocking reps
  are themselves the higher-risk condition
  [zhao-2024-block-jump-anticipated-unanticipated] (15 elite male
  players: the direction transfers, the magnitudes do not).
- **Observable promotion criterion ([[16s]]).** On a wide tempo set, the
  hands cross the plane of the net and are still before hitter contact,
  with no second reach, on 8 of 10 attempts. GMS names the failure as the
  double move, reaching high and then over, which puts the hands in
  position after the ball has passed
  [gms-2025-swing-blocking-everything-you-need-to-know]. Drills
  [[15s-drills]] and [[16s-drills]], scored [[tryout-rubric-15s]] and
  [[tryout-rubric-16u]].

### 17-18: Performance

- **Refine.** Swing blocking is the mandatory default at [[17s]] rather
  than an overlay [aoc-2024-importance-teaching-swing-blocking]. At
  [[18s]], bunch-read runs its full variant tree against scouted
  tendencies: multiple quick tempos, cross-court pin support,
  rotation-specific pinch depth [aoc-2026-bunch-read-blocking]. The
  advanced-level band Slabe names as bunch-read's natural home opens here.
- **Autonomy.** The middle reads and goes where the read sends her
  instead of closing mechanically to the pin blocker
  [aoc-2015-mccutcheon-blocking-keys]. Tactics come from the game plan
  with signals as a supplement; McGown preferred pulling the libero or
  setter over to relay information to the block
  [gms-2025-indoor-blocking-signals].
- **College preparation includes the priority order.** GMS reports
  blocking low in the skills-correlated-with-winning ranking, behind
  first-ball sideout, transition offense, and passing
  [gms-2025-blocking-importance]. The counter is a measurement argument:
  the block statistic counts only blocks that produce a point, so touches
  that keep a ball playable never enter the correlation
  [gms-2025-block-touches-vs-digs-stat]. An [[18s]] athlete headed to a
  college program should hear the ranking and the caveat.
- **Observable promotion criterion ([[18s]]).** Across one match of
  film, the blocker's move matches the set she read on 8 of 10 opponent
  attacks, counting a correct decline (staying home on a set that was not
  hers) as a match. McGown's expectation-setting bounds the standard:
  blocking stays messy against a team that passes well, and eliminating
  every hole in the block is not the target
  [gms-2025-indoor-blocking-signals]. Drills [[17s-drills]] and
  [[18s-drills]], scored [[tryout-rubric-17s]] and [[tryout-rubric-18u]].

## Schools of thought

- **[[gold-medal-squared]]**: motor-learning orthodoxy applied to
  blocking: named footwork lexicon, bunch-read as default system of 25+
  years, random/variable practice for reading transfer, early learning
  that "allows athletes to be late" so mechanics lock in before speed is
  demanded.
- **[[art-of-coaching-volleyball]]**: pedagogically eclectic, reading-
  centric, swing-blocking-as-modern-default-at-HS-and-above; Luka Slabe,
  Hugh McCutcheon, Karch Kiraly, and Matt Rockwell as the dominant
  voices, with Terry Liskevych and Ken Murczek as in-house dissenters on
  swing blocking specifically.
- **[[ecological-dynamics]]**: perception-action coupling; the blocker
  picks up affordances from the setter/hitter system in situ rather than
  processing discrete cues; training implication is preserve
  representative task constraints over drilling eye-targets in isolation.
- **[[japanese-training]]**: shorter-blocker tradition: timing, hand
  work, precise feet compensating for reach; the block is one layer of a
  defense-first system that expects to dig swings off the block, not
  necessarily stuff them.

## Getting started

If you are installing or upgrading blocking in your program, read in this
order:

1. **[[blocking-read]]** first. Reading is the primary skill; footwork
   and hand position without a read just move the block to the wrong
   place fast. The B-S-B-H eye-progression is where most programs
   underinvest.
2. **[[blocking-hand-position]]** next, where consensus is cleanest and
   the fewest coaching hours yield the most KPI improvement.
3. **[[blocking-footwork]]**: the generational decision point. Static
   shuffle and crossover come first as literacy; the swing layer goes on
   top once your program has the coaching hours.
4. **[[blocking-swing]]**: read alongside footwork so you treat the
   swing as an acceleration layer over static literacy.
5. **`## Teaching by age band`** above for the band-by-band install
   order and the promotion criteria, then **[[age-lens-14u]]**,
   **[[age-lens-hs]]**, and **[[age-lens-college]]** for the wider level
   overlays around it.

## Related areas

- **[[middle-blocker]]**: the position most defined by this skill;
  crossover footwork and commit-versus-read decisions live here.
- **[[outside-hitter]]**: pin blocking mechanics and seam seals from
  the left side.
- **[[opposite]]**: right-side blocking against the opposing team's
  primary outside, often the biggest block-transition-hit workload on
  the roster.
- **[[defense]]**: the block-defense coupling: what the block sets up
  for the back-row dig determines the whole team-defense scheme.
- **[[systems]]**: bunch-read, dedicated, and commit blocking schemes
  as team systems.
- **[[practice-planning]]**: where the training-hours tradeoff lives;
  swing blocking is expensive to install, and that cost is real.
