# Passing

## Overview

On the skill spine this hub is the serve receive category: the first contact off the opponent's serve, distinct from floor defense, which handles attacked balls and lives on [[defense]].

Passing is the first-contact skill that decides whether your offense gets to run. In modern women's indoor volleyball, passing quality is one of the two or three variables that correlates most tightly with sideout percentage, and at the club and high-school levels it is the single biggest determinant of whether a team can compete with more physical opponents. If you cannot pass, you cannot run tempo; if you cannot run tempo, you lose to any team that can.

Four contact types live under this skill. The forearm platform is the default for serves and hard-driven balls below the shoulders. The overhead pass, hands, not a set, handles serves above the shoulders and is dominant in the men's game and growing in the women's. Serve-receive names the team-layer problem: which passers cover which zones, which formation you run, who hides. Free-ball passing sits apart, a slower feed from across the net that your team should convert into offense at the highest conversion rate of any first contact.

The modern teaching consensus is that passing is **a reading skill executed with a platform**. The older order, "get the platform right first, then worry about reading", produces passers who look clean against coach tosses and fall apart against real serves. The modern reverse, read the server, move the feet, form the platform late, produces passers whose technique holds up at game speed. How much scaffolding a beginning passer needs before the live-serve work begins is where the schools genuinely disagree.

The developmental overlays live on [[age-lens-14u]], [[age-lens-hs]], and [[age-lens-college]]. The 14U conversation is mostly about how much platform work a young passer can absorb before game-like reps begin; HS and college are mostly about formation choice, passer specialization, and serving-environment intensity.

## Major subtopics

- **[[passing-forearm]]**: the two-arm platform contact. The workhorse of serve-receive and first-contact defense. Covers posture, pursuit, platform formation, reading the server, and the common errors (reaching with the arms, pre-forming the platform, passing off the hip, active arm swing at contact). The most contested teaching topic in volleyball fundamentals.
- **[[passing-overhead]]**: hands used as a pass, not a set, on balls above the shoulders. Dominant in the men's game, rising in the women's game as servers get bigger and contact points get higher. Requires clean setting hands plus the judgment to choose overhead over forearm under time pressure.
- **[[passing-serve-receive]]**: the team layer: two-passer vs. three-passer vs. four-passer formations, who hides, libero-positioning rules, how the formation flexes by rotation. Where individual technique meets rotational constraints.
- **[[passing-free-ball]]**: a slower feed from across the net that should always be converted to quick offense. A distinct training emphasis from serve-receive because the decision and transition windows are different.

## Schools of thought

Four positions sit on passing methodology, with the sharpest contested ground being how much decomposed technique work to do before live-serve passing begins. The skill-level treatment here mirrors the four-way framing on [[passing-forearm]]; the per-page references carry the operational detail.

**[[gold-medal-squared]].** Motor-learning orthodoxy. Build the movement pattern first so attention is free for reading, use random and variable practice over blocked repetition, keep the drill library small, coach from external-focus cues. The empirical anchor is the contextual-interference effect, random practice looks worse in the acquisition phase but dramatically better in retention and transfer. GMS builds technique inside game-like practice rather than building it first and inserting it into games.

**[[art-of-coaching-volleyball]].** Reading-first teaching in the posture-pursuit-platform order. Kiraly's four keys to the forearm pass, stopped and balanced, thumbs and wrists together, set the angle with the hips, one simple one-way motion to the target, is the canonical frame. Bechard's posture-pursuit-platform names the component order. The AOC line is consistent: the passer's eyes, feet, and posture resolve first; the platform forms at the last moment to match what the eyes and feet have already committed to.

**[[japanese-training]].** Passing sits inside a defensive identity: dig up everything, recover to ready after every contact, treat each first-contact as a point-saving act. The pedagogical principle is deliberate repetition with purpose, high-volume work until the response is autonomic, precision as program standard, midline emphasis. Modern Japanese youth practice is less severe than the Daimatsu-era model but retains the same technical targets.

**[[ecological-dynamics]].** The theoretical opposition to decomposed-then-recombined teaching. Practice tasks must preserve the information-movement coupling of competition, a passing drill off coach tosses is not representatively designed because it decouples the passer from the serve-read information a real server carries. The constraints-led coach does not dictate technique; the coach engineers the constraints under which effective technique self-organizes. GMS and ecological dynamics agree practice must look like the game; they disagree on whether skill is a motor program the athlete acquires or an emergent athlete-environment relationship.

Practical reconciliation: the operational practice designs converge more than the rhetoric suggests. Everyone agrees you should train passing against live serves at game speed, early and often. Everyone agrees tossed-ball decomposition produces clean acquisition and poor transfer. The scaffolding question is per-player, not settled in the literature.

## Teaching by age band

Single-year precision lives in the age-guides and the tryout rubrics. These cells carry band-level shape and point at the year that owns each detail.

### 10-12: Foundation

- **Introduce.** Platform shape (flat arms, wrists and thumbs together, arms off the chest, knees bent, shoulders over the front foot) [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals], shuffle-not-reach pursuit [aoc-2023-passing-shuffle-not-reach], one printed target the whole gym passes to. See [[passing-forearm]], [[passing-cues]].
- **Develop.** Kiraly's four keys as a single bundle from 11s rather than one key per year [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass], angle to target set by the shoulders and hips.
- **Refine.** Nothing yet. This band buys the pattern; polish is a Rebuild-band job.
- **Year gates. 10s:** light balls (pink or Baden Softlights) off tossed feeds, and reading the server is 14U-tier work [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals]. See [[10s]]. **11s:** four keys bundled, "aim with your shoulders" the working short form [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass]. See [[11s]]. **12s:** four-player serve receive, seam responsibility, mine/yours/out, and a defined target rather than "high enough to set"; the overhead contact stays a free-ball tool, since overhead reception of a serve is 14U-and-up material [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals]. See [[12s]], [[passing-overhead]].
- **Observable promotion criterion.** Out of 10 tossed reps, 7 meet the 7 foot target (peak at least seven feet, arriving inside the six foot setter box), angle visibly coming from the shoulders and hips rather than a lifted arm. Scored on [[tryout-rubric-12s]].
- **Drill entry.** GMS's youth passing circle initiates from the far side of the net so the ball crosses it the way a serve does, starting passers in the three back row receiving positions [gms-2026-passing-circle-youth-variation]. [[butterfly-passing]] carries volume; pick list at [[12s-drills]].

### 13-14: Rebuild

- **Introduce.** Pass grading. 13s is where the 0 to 3 anchors get called aloud [aoc-2020-dean-passing-stats]: 3 is a perfect pass with every attack option open, 2 is hittable to outside or right side, 1 is out of system, 0 is an ace or overpass. The anchors are defined once on [[passing-serve-receive]] and every year above points there.
- **Reporting alternative.** GMS argues for good pass percentage (the share of attempts graded good or perfect) over a decimal average: real-time grading is hard to keep consistent mid-rally, and young players cannot read the gap between a 2.05 and a 1.95. GMS reports good-pass-percentage rank matching passing-average rank almost exactly across USA Women's National Team, University of Washington, and high school data [gms-2025-passing-statistics]. Either number works here as long as the gym keeps one.
- **Develop.** Late platform [aoc-2018-stone-platform-management], quiet platform and pass-and-cover as named drill targets [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing], and three-person serve receive as the base ([[serve-receive-3-player]]).
- **Refine.** Pursuit distance and contact height, both of which move during peak height velocity. A passer whose arms grew four inches over the club season contacts the ball in a place her feet learned last year.
- **Year gates. 13s:** overhead passing on high balls and free balls, with full hand-passing on serve receive still 14U-and-up [aoc-2024-kids-passing-fundamentals]. See [[13s]]. **14s:** the overhead-versus-forearm choice becomes a deliberate read, and the hop-back on a jamming ball replaces the chest-reach [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing]. See [[14s]], [[age-lens-14u]].
- **Observable promotion criterion.** Sixty percent or more of receptions off a moderate-pace standing float graded 2 or better, with the pass-and-cover sprint visible after contact instead of a passer admiring her pass [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing]. Scored on [[tryout-rubric-13s]] and [[tryout-rubric-14u]].

**Contested cell: pre-contact movement and one uniform technique. Program default pending.** This band is where the disagreement changes how you run a rep block.

- **Prescriptive position.** Pick one passing technique and teach it across the whole gym: same contact, same pre-contact posture, same platform mechanics, and no passer gets a different cue set than her teammate [aoc-2024-kiraly-serve-receive-unit]. On movement, [[passing-serve-receive]] tells the passer to start moving before the ball crosses the net, and Kiraly's first key asks her to be stopped and balanced before contact [aoc-2013-kiraly-4-keys-forearm-pass].
- **GMS position.** Refuse the prescription. Pre-contact movement sometimes helps and sometimes interferes, and the physics analogy (an object in motion stays in motion) fails because a passer is a perception-action system rather than a passive object. Great passers move in different ways: subtle reactive weight shifts for some, a rocker step, drop step, or hop for others, and players who look still are already shifting tension and attention. The goal is functional variability and adaptive stability, and the test is outcome based [gms-2025-should-players-move-before-contact]. GMS extends the reasoning to contact point, calling "pick a side" a cue teams teach and then contradict on their own video, and concluding that passers need to be good on the left, on the right, and on the midline [gms-2026-thought-provoking-passing-question].
- **Shared ground.** Reading the server belongs early: GMS puts tracking, stated as "see server, see spin," in its week one preseason keys [gms-2025-preseason-week-1-1-passing].

Settle the program rule before your first Rebuild-band rep block; the two positions produce different feedback on the same shank.

### 15-16: Refinement

- **Introduce.** Libero-split as the default formation at 16s, with three-person as the rotation-specific alternative when two strong passers flank the libero [aoc-2024-sr-formations]. See [[serve-receive-libero-split]].
- **Develop.** Overhead reception against jump-floats that arrive above the shoulder ([[passing-overhead]]), and a serve-receive identity assigned to a libero plus two to three reliable back row outsides [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing].
- **Refine.** The ball source rather than the number. The 2.0 target does not move from 14s to 15s, so the rising standard is carried by the serve behind it going from standing float to live jump-float [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing].
- **Year gates. 15s:** 2.0 or better pass-grade average for primary passers, 2.2 for libero candidates carrying split-receive responsibility [aoc-2024-sr-formations]. See [[15s]]. **16s:** 2.0 mid-season at a typical national club and 2.2 to 2.4 in an elite cohort [aoc-2024-kiraly-stats-matter-most]; passing reps leave passing-only blocks for wash and 6v6 [aoc-2025-beal-josephson-dont-train-passing-isolation]; call early and call loud stops being coach-prompted [aoc-2024-sr-team-communication]. See [[16s]], [[age-lens-hs]].
- **Observable promotion criterion.** 2.0 or better across a full live-serve receive block against jump-float, seam call audible before the ball crosses the net on every rep. [[15s]] also asks the athlete to verbalize a B-S-B-H read sequence on request [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill]; keep that as a diagnostic rather than the gate, since an athlete can say a sequence back correctly and still run a different intention on the rep [trinsey-2026-good-intentions].
- **Drill entry.** The GMS serve-receive ladder splits the court and plays both directions for four simultaneous games, rotating server, passer, and target; its promotion and relegation layer ranks passers as a byproduct, which makes it a tryout instrument as well as a rep drill [gms-2025-serve-receive-ladder]. Scored on [[tryout-rubric-15s]]; pick list at [[16s-drills]].

### 17-18: Performance

- **Introduce.** The four-passer adjustment as a player-called lever against exceptional topspin servers, callable in-rotation without a coach prompt [aoc-2024-sr-formations].
- **Develop.** Forearm and overhead as co-equal tools, co-developed rather than sequenced, with Bechard's square-tilt pursuit-ready posture visible on every match-pace rep [aoc-2025-bechard-square-tilt-weight-transfer].
- **Refine.** What the grade measures. At 18s the question is whether the setter ran the offense she pre-called, so the grade attaches to the offense the pass produced rather than to contact location alone. [[18s]] carries the tournament-average target.
- **Year gates. 17s:** libero-split is the default with the four-passer look as the scouted alternative [aoc-2024-sr-formations], free-ball passing goes reflexively to a high-2 or a tempo trigger (see [[17s]]), and "this ball is yours unless you call somebody off it" is the accountability standard [aoc-2026-lingenfelter-accountability-passing]. See [[passing-free-ball]]. **18s:** pressure rehearsal in serve receive is non-optional, because reading degrades under pressure and shows up in passing first [aoc-2024-kiraly-reads-every-skill]. See [[18s]], [[age-lens-college]].
- **Observable promotion criterion.** 2.0 or better across full-match samples with the read-then-form sequence visible (the platform arrives late), plus the formation adjustment called and executed mid-set with no bench prompt. Scored on [[tryout-rubric-17s]]; pick list at [[18s-drills]].
- **Reporting.** Good pass percentage stays usable here, validated against passing average on USA Women's National Team and University of Washington data [gms-2025-passing-statistics]. [[libero]] carries the position standard; [[blitz-the-bro]] is the libero-zone stress test.

## Getting started

If you are upgrading how you teach passing, read in this order:

1. [[passing-forearm]]: the full teaching progression, common errors, and the four-way schools-perspectives framing. The longest and most cited page in this area.
2. [[passing-serve-receive]]: the team layer once individual technique is in place.
3. [[passing-overhead]]: add the overhead contact once the forearm platform is stable; it is a mid-progression skill.
4. [[passing-free-ball]]: a separate drill emphasis from serve-receive; do not let it get buried inside "transition" work.
5. Your age-lens page, [[age-lens-14u]], [[age-lens-hs]], or [[age-lens-college]]: for the developmentally appropriate teaching load.
6. [[block-vs-random-practice]] if you want the methodology evidence base underneath the schools disagreement.

For drill entry, the library is catalogued on the technique pages. [[butterfly-passing]] is the cooperative baseline; [[queen-of-the-court-passing]] scales to competitive; [[serve-receive-3v3]] layers in the rotational constraint; [[pepper]] and its partner variants handle defensive touches adjacent to passing.
