# Tryout Rubric: 15s

## Purpose

This rubric translates the [[15s]] `## Promotion criteria` section into a 1-5 scoring scale a coaching staff can apply consistently across a single 60-90 minute tryout session. Use it to evaluate athletes graduating from [[14s]] into the 15s specialization year, and to triangulate which 15s players are tracking 16s-ready by season end. The rubric is calibrated against the [[age-lens-hs]] parent context, most 15s tryout candidates are simultaneously HS freshmen on a JV or varsity roster, and the operational standards there are the standards a serious 15s club trains to.

The rubric's design unifying-principle: **Column 5 (next-age-ready) describes a 16s starting-roster baseline.** A player scoring 5 across most rows is ready to start at 16s today. A player scoring 3 across most rows is age-appropriate at 15s. Below 2 sustained across multiple rows indicates the player is still tracking 14s milestones and may need a developmental rather than competitive 15s placement.

**Column 5 of every row is the next age's column-3 standard; it flags readiness, it is not install content for this age.** Every column-5 cell below is written against the [[tryout-rubric-16u]] column-3 anchors so the two rubrics dovetail at the seam. A 15s athlete who scores 5 has already reached the 16s bar; that is a placement signal, and it is not a licence to teach 16s content to the 15s group.

Pass grades throughout use the 0-3 scale whose anchors are defined once at [[passing-serve-receive]]. Do not re-anchor the scale locally, and note that an unchanged grade across age bands is a rising standard, because the serve gets harder every year.

## Evaluation criteria

## Scoring

**Bands are row averages, not sums.** Score all eight rows 1-5, then divide by the number of rows scored. Averages are used here specifically so that position weighting and any skipped row leave the placement bands intact; a raw sum does not survive either.

- **4.25+:** 16s starting-roster ready. Consider varsity-track placement and accelerated curriculum.
- **3.4 to 4.24:** Tracking 16s by season end. Standard 15s competitive placement.
- **2.5 to 3.39:** Age-appropriate 15s. Standard 15s placement with development emphasis on the 1-2 weakest rows.
- **1.9 to 2.49:** 14s milestones still resolving. Consider developmental 15s, second team, or extended 14s if program structure allows.
- **Below 1.9:** Not yet 15s-ready. Direct conversation with player and family about appropriate placement.

**Position weighting, done so the bands still hold.** Weighting is acceptable and encouraged: for a libero candidate double-weight Passing and Defense; for a setter candidate double-weight Setting and Mental; for a middle candidate double-weight Blocking and Hitting. Compute the **weighted average**: multiply each row by its weight, sum, then divide by the sum of the weights (ten, not eight, when two rows are doubled). Dividing by eight after doubling two rows inflates the result by roughly a quarter and pushes mid-band athletes into the band above; that arithmetic error is the reason these bands are expressed as averages at all. Document the weighting before tryouts begin so it is consistent across evaluators.

**Non-setters.** Setter is the only position-conditional row. A non-setter candidate scores the other seven rows and averages over seven; do not enter a zero for Setting and do not average over eight.

## Calibration notes

**Session structure.** A 60-90 minute tryout cannot evaluate eight criteria with equal depth. Run a 15-minute warmup (observe Athletic profile / movement quality without scoring), 20 minutes of skill stations (Passing / Setting / Hitting rotated through), 20 minutes of small-sided play (Defense / Transition / Blocking observed in 3v3 or 4v4), and 20 minutes of 6v6 wash drill (Mental / coachability foregrounded; cross-validate skill scores under live conditions). Score during 6v6, not during stations, stations isolate technique but the rubric describes game performance.

**Multi-evaluator consistency.** Two evaluators per court minimum. Score independently, then reconcile rows where you differ by 2+ points before averaging. The most common inter-rater disagreement at 15s is on Mental & coachability, anchor it to the observable behaviors in the rubric (does she own the rep? does she articulate the read? does she reset between attempts?), not to evaluator gut-feel.

**Common biases to avoid.** Early-maturer halo (a tall, physically mature 15s often scores high on athletic eye-test but row-3 on Mental); recency bias (the last drill block is overweighted); position-mismatch bias (a libero-track athlete evaluated against an OH-shaped rubric will under-score). The rubric's 1-5 scale describes observable behaviors at each level, not coach impressions; if the column-3 description fits, score 3.

**ACL-mechanism red flags** override scoring. If an athlete demonstrates run-back block landings with knee-valgus collapse, flag for the ACL-prevention curriculum regardless of her average. Mechanism risk is a coaching priority, not a placement decision. See [[sassi-2025-acl-volleyball-systematic-review]] and [[zahradnik-2014-block-landing-grf-valgus]].

**Serving is the row evaluators most often mis-score.** The program curriculum is float-dominant through 15s with topspin held to 16+, so a topspin attempt is not evidence of an advanced server here and must not be rewarded in columns 4 or 5. What earns column 4 is jump-float placement (70%+ in-zone with a short-deep mix on demand); what earns column 5 is that placement holding at match pace, to small targets, under score pressure. An athlete who can only serve topspin is scored on what her float does.
